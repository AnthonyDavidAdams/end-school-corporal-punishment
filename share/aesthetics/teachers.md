# Teachers and aides in paddling districts

**Where they see images:** teacher Facebook groups and subreddits, the staff lounge wall, Teachers Pay Teachers aesthetics, planner and lanyard culture, the union newsletter, Instagram teacher accounts.

**Visual language they trust:** classroom-decor aesthetic done well: bulletin-board borders, chalk and marker textures, sticky notes, index cards, the checklist and the anchor chart. Friendly rounded sans and a hand-lettered accent (not Comic Sans, not Papyrus), bright but controlled palettes (mustard, teal, coral on white), the "poster on the wall" format. Practical always beats inspirational: a script, a checklist, a thing to say at 8:05.

**What reads as an outsider:** telling teachers they are the problem; anything implying they hit kids for fun; corporate wellness gloss; edu-jargon without a tool attached.

**Copy voice:** colleague to colleague. Name the fear (losing the room) and give the tool. Teachers do not administer the paddle in most policies; principals do, on the teacher's referral. The ask: "Here is what to do instead, free, on one page." Lead with Module 2's five routines and the two-step correction.

**Formats:** 8.5x11 anchor-chart poster; 1080x1080; 4x6 lanyard card (the five questions).

**Concepts that fit:**
- Anchor chart: "THE TWO-STEP CORRECTION" with the script, in classroom-poster style.
- Sticky-note grid: "5 things that cut referrals by a third" (the Bradshaw PBIS finding, year cited).
- Lanyard card: the five repair questions.
- "Nobody wants to be the one holding it." with an empty principal's chair; the training link.
