# Design language briefs

One brief per audience we need to move. Each describes where that audience actually sees images, the visual conventions they trust, what reads as an outsider's work, and the copy voice that lands. The `meme-kit` skill reads the brief for the audience it is given and generates art and type inside those conventions.

Audiences are defined by role and setting, never by race or religion as a target. The briefs describe communities' own visual languages so that the work looks like it belongs on their wall, not like a campaign that flew in.

| Brief | Audience | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| `rural-parents.md` | Parents in the small Southern and Midwestern districts that still paddle | Facebook community groups, the school pickup line, the county fair |
| `congregations.md` | Church members and pastors in those districts | Bulletin inserts, fellowship-hall bulletin boards, church Facebook pages |
| `teachers.md` | Teachers and aides in paddling districts | Teacher Facebook groups, the staff lounge, planner and lanyard culture |
| `board-members.md` | School board members and superintendents | The board packet, the local paper, LinkedIn, the Rotary lunch |
| `legislators.md` | State legislators and staff in the 15 states | The one-pager left on the desk, the district newsletter, local TV |
| `former-students.md` | Adults 18 to 35 who were paddled, and their peers | Instagram, TikTok, Reddit, group chats |

## Rules for every piece

1. **Every number comes from `facts/claims/` with its year**, printed on the piece. No number without a year.
2. **Sign it.** Every piece carries "earthpilot.org/kids" or the repository name. Nothing is made to look like it came from a church, a school, a district, a party, or a local group. Borrowing a community's visual language is design; borrowing its name is fraud.
3. **No students.** No photographs or realistic renderings of children being struck, crying, or identifiable. Objects, hands, empty rooms, typography, and adults are all fine.
4. **No AI tells.** Extra fingers, melted text, uncanny faces. Prefer typography-forward pieces with generated art as texture or a single object; set all type locally so the words and numbers are exact.
5. **No emoji.** Type marks, rules, and imagery only.
6. **No contempt.** The people in these audiences are the ones we need. Nothing that mocks the South, faith, rural life, teachers, or parents who were themselves paddled.
7. **One idea per piece.** A number, a line, a place to go.
