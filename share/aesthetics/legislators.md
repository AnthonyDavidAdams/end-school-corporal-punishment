# State legislators and staff

**Where they see images:** the one-pager on the desk before the hearing, the committee packet, local TV, the district newsletter, the constituent letter with a photo attached, the state school boards and superintendents associations' bulletins.

**Visual language they trust:** the legislative one-pager: state seal colors, a bold headline, three bullets, one map or table, sources in small type. The map of their own state by district is the single most effective image, especially when it shows that most districts already stopped. Bipartisan cues: the Republican sponsor from the neighboring state, the governor's parental-rights quote.

**What reads as an outsider:** anything from a national organization's brand, international comparisons up front, federal framing (the bill they would vote for is a state bill), emotional photography.

**Copy voice:** local control, parental rights, liability, order, and precedent from a state they respect. Four sentences and the ask. The New Mexico and Idaho case studies are the templates: a Republican governor's words and a Republican sponsor's bill.

**Formats:** 8.5x11 one-pager; the state district map as a standalone image; a 4x6 card for the desk.

**Concepts that fit:**
- The state map: districts that prohibit (most of them) versus permit, with counts, "Your district stopped. This bill protects the rest."
- "What Idaho did in 2023" card: bill number, sponsor's party, votes, what it also covered.
- The opt-in card: "Louisiana, Missouri and Florida require a parent's signature first. [STATE] does not."
