# School board members and superintendents

**Where they see images:** the board packet, the local newspaper (print and Facebook), LinkedIn, the state school boards association magazine, the Rotary or Chamber lunch, the insurance renewal.

**Visual language they trust:** the one-page brief. White paper, navy and gray, a serif headline (Georgia) over a clean sans body, a single chart in one color, a table, a footer with sources. The district's own logo colors when possible. It should look like something the superintendent's office produced, without claiming to be. Nothing clever; competence is the aesthetic.

**What reads as an outsider:** activism vocabulary, hashtags, a call to "join the movement," national statistics without local ones, any hint of a pile-on.

**Copy voice:** risk, order, and comparison to peers. "Districts like ours that stopped" with names. The district's own numbers. The insurance question. The three asks from the district playbook in order: agenda item, honor every refusal, publish the count.

**Formats:** 8.5x11 one-pager (front only); a single chart image for the newspaper.

**Concepts that fit:**
- "[DISTRICT] and its neighbors": a table of nearby districts, status, and students struck, with sources.
- One chart: students struck in the district by year from the CRDC, with the neighboring district's line at zero.
- "Three questions for our insurer" card.
- "What the last four districts to stop did next" (Kentucky, Robeson, Graham, Hendry) as a brief.
