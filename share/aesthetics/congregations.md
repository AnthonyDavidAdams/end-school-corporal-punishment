# Congregations and pastors in paddling districts

**Where they see images:** the Sunday bulletin insert, the fellowship-hall bulletin board, the church Facebook page, the women's ministry group chat, the youth pastor's Instagram.

**Visual language they trust:** the bulletin insert and the VBS flyer. Warm cream and kraft paper, a single accent color (deep red, forest green, denim blue), classic serifs (Georgia, Baskerville) for scripture-cadence sentences and a clean sans for details, thin rules and small caps, a single simple line illustration (a lamp, hands, an open door), lots of white space. Quiet and dignified; nothing shouting.

**What reads as an outsider:** arguing scripture, quoting the rod verses either way, secular-authority framing ("the research says," "the American Academy of..."), anything that looks like it is correcting the church.

**Copy voice:** the language of care and stewardship: children entrusted, gentleness, the adult who stays calm. Never tell a congregation what the Bible means. Let a pastor or a church member in the district be the voice; the piece is a frame for their words. The ask is a conversation, not a vote: "Ask your school what happens instead."

**Formats:** 5.5x8.5 bulletin insert (half letter); 1080x1080 for the church page; 11x17 poster for the fellowship hall.

**Concepts that fit:**
- Cream card, one serif sentence from a local pastor (with permission, name and church), a line rule, "Ask your school what it does instead. earthpilot.org/kids".
- "The calmest adult in the room" series: a plain object (a chair, a cup of coffee, an open door) and one line about what a teacher does instead of hitting.
- A bulletin insert listing the neighboring districts that stopped, in the style of the prayer list.
