# Concept

**Working title:** A Christian's Guide to Corporal Punishment
**Subtitle:** The rod, the fruit, and the school board
**Author:** Anthony Adams (a former ordained minister; the booklet's first-person voice)
**Format:** a tract. Four by six inches, thirty-two pages, black ink on cream, one drawn scene facing each idea.

**One-sentence premise.** Scripture itself tells a believer how to judge any discipline, by its fruit, and by that test the paddle fails.

**Reader.** A pastor, a Sunday school teacher, a church member, or the believer who sits on a school board in one of the seventeen states where a public school employee may still paddle a child. Someone who will be handed this in a fellowship hall or a board-meeting lobby and read it in ten minutes.

**Reader promise.** You will not be asked to doubt the Bible. You will be shown the rod verses in full, told honestly what they say, and then shown what the same Bible says about judging what we do with them, with the research as the fruit.

**Author purpose.** The Safe Schools Project's persuasion playbook says faith is the frame most board dossiers show, and that scripture is argued by local pastors, not by the campaign. This is the thing to hand those pastors and the deacon on the board. The campaign's conversion is a one-sentence policy; the tract ends on that sentence.

**Unique angle.** Written from inside scripture by someone who has preached from it; quotes every rod verse rather than explaining them away; every figure traceable to a verified claim file; every church position read at the church's own site.

**Differentiator (Halpern's six).** Big idea: the fruit test. Why this author: a former minister with fifteen years on the question. What the reader craves: permission to vote no without feeling they voted against the Bible. Authority: the facts registry behind every sentence. Visibility: the campaign's site and the kit. Pitch: "Same scripture. Different board."

**Emotional arc.** Wary (am I being asked to doubt scripture?) to reassured (the verses are all here) to convinced (the fruit runs one way) to equipped (the one sentence, the vote).

**Source.** `kit/a-christians-guide.md`, about 4,800 words. The tract keeps the booklet's order and voice and cuts to about 1,500 words plus the 396-word read-aloud page.
