# Replacing corporal punishment: an open training for schools

The single most effective argument for keeping corporal punishment is "then what do we do instead?" This is the answer: a free, open curriculum a principal can adopt on a Monday, built only on practices with published evidence, and honest about where that evidence is thin.

It is written for the schools that still paddle: small, often rural, often under-resourced districts in the South and Midwest where the average school has no behavior specialist and the principal is the discipline system. Nothing here requires a consultant, a purchase, or a new position.

## Who it is for

- Principals and assistant principals who administer discipline
- Teachers who refer students for discipline
- School boards voting on a policy change
- District staff writing the transition plan

## How it is organized

| 4 | Repair: restorative conversations that work in five minutes | 2 hrs | All staff |
| 5 | The 5 percent: students with repeated referrals | 3 hrs | Leadership team, counselors |
| 6 | Students with disabilities and trauma histories | 2 hrs | All staff |
| 7 | Parents: the letter, the meeting, the opt-out, the ally | 1 hr | Administrators |
| 8 | Data: the referral log, the monthly review, the public report | 1 hr | Leadership team |
| 9 | Facilitator guide and slide notes | | Whoever runs the training |

Each module in `modules/` has: what it replaces, the evidence (with citations to `evidence.md`), the practice, a script or checklist, a one-page handout, and what to do when it is not working.

## Principles

1. **Evidence or silence.** Every practice cites a study in `evidence.md`, with its effect size and its limits. Where a widely used practice lacks good evidence, the module says so and offers it as optional.
2. **Nothing to buy.** Free, public-domain or openly licensed materials only. Where a commercial program has the best evidence, the module names it and describes the free core practice it shares.
3. **Small-school first.** Written for a principal with 300 students and no counselor, then scaled up, not down from a suburban district.
4. **Replaces a practice with a practice.** Every module answers the question "the student did X; what do I do now?" with a concrete sequence.
5. **Honest about time.** Prevention is cheaper than paddling; it is not free. The 90-day plan is explicit about hours.
