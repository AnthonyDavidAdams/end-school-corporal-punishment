# Module 3: The office: what happens instead of the paddle

**Time:** three hours to build the ladder; posted in every room. **Audience:** principal, assistant principal, anyone who receives referrals.

## What this module replaces

"Three licks or three days." The ladder is a published sequence of responses that every administrator uses the same way, so that students, teachers and parents can predict what happens, and so that the school stops choosing between the paddle and suspension.

## The evidence

- School-wide PBIS, which is this ladder plus the routines in Module 2 plus the data in Module 8, reduced suspensions and office referrals in a five-year randomized trial across 37 schools; children in PBIS schools were 33 percent less likely to be referred (`../evidence.md` B1).
- Consistency across administrators is the fidelity component the trials measured. A ladder used by one administrator and not another is not the intervention.
- The risk of swapping paddling for suspension is real and undocumented in the research (B9). The ladder is designed so that suspension is the last rung, not the second.

## The ladder

Post it. Every rung names who does it, how long it takes, and what the student does next.

| Rung | For | Response | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First minor referral | Re-teach the expectation, five-minute reset in the office, back to class with a note home | Any administrator or the counselor |
| 2 | Repeated minor | Reflection sheet (what happened, who was affected, what I will do next time), a repair task from Module 4, parent call by the student with the administrator present | Administrator |
| 3 | Third minor in a month, or first major (fighting, property, defiance that stopped class) | Same-day parent meeting, restorative conversation with the person affected (Module 4), loss of a privilege tied to the behavior, check-in/check-out for two weeks (Module 5) | Administrator plus teacher |
| 4 | Repeated major | Behavior plan (Module 5), in-school placement with work, daily check-in, weekly parent contact | Leadership team |
| 5 | Safety threat: weapons, serious assault, drugs | Out-of-school suspension per code, with a re-entry meeting on the first day back and a plan from Module 5 | Principal |

Rules that make it work:

- **Nobody skips rungs** except for rung 5 offenses. A teacher who wants rung 3 for a rung 1 behavior gets a conversation, not a suspension.
- **Every rung ends with a return to class and a repair,** because the lesson is that the student belongs here and owes something, not that they are gone.
- **Every rung is logged** on the referral form with the rung number (Module 8). The monthly review looks at whether the rungs are being used as written.
- **The ladder is in the handbook and in the parent letter** (Module 7), in plain language.

## Script: rung 2, the parent call

> "Mrs. Ortiz, this is Devon. I'm in Mr. Hale's office. I want to tell you what happened today." (Student explains. Administrator adds only facts.) "The plan is that I will [repair task] and Mr. Hale will check with Ms. Bell on Friday. Is there anything you want Mr. Hale to know?"

## When it is not working

- If the office is full at rung 1, the classroom routines are not in place; go back to Module 2 rather than adding rungs.
- If one administrator's students climb the ladder faster than another's, that is the fidelity problem the research warns about. The leadership team reviews it monthly by name and rung, and the fix is coaching, not policy.
