# Module 5: The 5 percent: students with repeated referrals

**Time:** three hours, then a weekly 20-minute team meeting. **Audience:** leadership team, counselor if any, the referring teachers.

## What this module replaces

Paddling the same students over and over. Every veteran teacher knows who those students were, which is the plainest evidence that the paddle did not work on them. This module is the plan for the three to five percent of students who generate most referrals.

## The evidence

- Check-In/Check-Out, a daily two-minute morning and afternoon contact with one adult plus a point card carried between classes, is the most studied Tier 2 practice; a systematic review calls its evidence base mixed but sufficient on some outcomes, mostly from single-case designs (`../evidence.md` B4). It is cheap, so its modest evidence is enough to make it the first thing to try.
- The Good Behavior Game's long-term effects were concentrated in the boys who were most aggressive in first grade (B3): universal practice reaches this group most.
- Collaborative and Proactive Solutions (Ross Greene) has randomized clinical evidence for oppositional children equal to parent training, but no whole-school trial (B7). Use its core idea, that the student lacks a skill rather than the will, as the frame for the plan meeting.
- The federal Office of Special Education Programs' 2016 letter states that when a student with an IEP has behavior that impedes learning, the IEP team must consider positive behavioral interventions (B10). Module 6 covers this.

## The practice

**Step 1: Identify.** Any student with three referrals in a month, or two majors, goes on the list. The referral log (Module 8) produces it automatically.

**Step 2: Check-In/Check-Out for two weeks.** Pick one adult the student does not dislike. Two minutes at arrival (card, goal for the day, one encouraging sentence), teacher marks the card each period on the posted expectations, two minutes at dismissal (points, praise for what went right, card home). Weekly graph on the wall of the office.

**Step 3: If the graph is flat after two weeks, hold the plan meeting.** Thirty minutes, student present for part of it, parent invited. Three questions: what is the behavior exactly (when, where, with whom); what does the student get or avoid by it; what skill would they need to not do it. Then one skill to teach, one adult to teach it, one change to the environment (seat, schedule, hallway route), one way the student can ask for a break without a scene, and one thing the parent will do. Written on one page.

**Step 4: Review weekly.** Twenty minutes, the whole list, the graphs. Plans that are working end after four good weeks; plans that are not change one element.

## Handout

The point card template, the one-page plan template, the weekly graph.

## When it is not working

- If the list is more than 10 percent of enrollment, the problem is universal (Modules 2 and 3), not individual.
- If the same student is on the list all year, the question is whether an evaluation for special education services is owed (Module 6) or whether the student needs something the school does not have; the counselor or the district's mental health referral path is the next step, and the plan says so.
