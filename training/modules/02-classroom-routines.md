# Module 2: Classroom routines that prevent most referrals

**Time:** two half-days of training plus 20 minutes a week of coaching for the first semester. **Audience:** every teacher and aide.

## What this module replaces

The threat. In a paddling school, most classroom management runs on the possibility of being sent to the office. Remove the paddle without replacing the threat with a routine and referrals climb. This module is the routine.

## The evidence

- Classroom management programs improve behavior, academics and social-emotional outcomes with an average effect of g = 0.22 across 54 controlled studies (`../evidence.md` B5, Korpershoek et al. 2016).
- The What Works Clearinghouse practice guide's two strong-evidence recommendations are: modify the classroom environment, and teach and reinforce new skills (B10).
- The Good Behavior Game, a free classroom routine, produced effects still measurable at ages 19 to 21 in randomized trials (B3, Kellam et al.).
- Teacher classroom-management training (Incredible Years) works in randomized trials, with small average effects that are larger for the students who start furthest behind (B5, Reinke et al. 2018).

## The practice: five routines

1. **Three to five posted expectations,** stated as what to do ("Hands and feet to yourself," "Voice level 1 during work"), taught in the first week with examples and practice, and re-taught after every break. The same expectations in every room and hallway in the building; that is the whole-school part (`03-office-response-ladder.md`).
2. **Transitions and arrivals scripted.** Most referrals come from the first five minutes, the last five, and the hallway. Write down what students do on entry (a task on the board, materials out), how the class moves, and how it ends. Practice it like a fire drill.
3. **Five positives to one correction.** Specific, quiet, and frequent: "Marcus, you started right away." The ratio is the single most replicated finding in the classroom management literature. Track it for a week with tally marks; nearly every teacher is surprised by their own number.
4. **The Good Behavior Game,** two or three times a week in grades K through 5. Teams, a short list of behaviors that lose a point, a brief reward for every team that stays under the limit. Free instructions from the PBIS Center and the evidence base in B3. Coaching matters: the replication cohort with less teacher support had smaller effects.
5. **A calm, private, two-step correction.** Step one: restate the expectation to the student, not the room, and move on. Step two: a choice with a consequence you control ("Work here or at the back table; either way the page is due at 10:15"). Only after both steps is it an office matter, and the referral form (`08-data.md`) asks which steps were used.

## Script: the first correction

> "Jada, voice level 1. Thanks." (Turn away. Do not wait for compliance with eye contact; give her ten seconds to save face.)

## Handout

One page: the five routines, the 5:1 tally sheet, and the Good Behavior Game one-page rules.

## When it is not working

- A teacher with referral counts far above the building average is not a discipline problem; they need the coaching visit, not a memo. Pair them with the teacher whose counts are lowest for two observation swaps.
- If referrals cluster in one period, the problem is the schedule or the task, not the students. Fix the transition.
