effectively; use appropriate social skills; and become a responsible individual.

with parents, families and community, accept the responsibility to ensure that all students meet or exceed
our established educational goals by maximizing opportunities for learning and recognizing the potential of
each student in our school, thus creating life-long learners.

Perfect Attendance awards are presented to students who are neither absent nor tardy (includes being
tardy between classes) during the year. Absences for school functions, including fair participation and
animal show participation, are allowed.

Excused Absences are still absences. Students are missing vital information when they are absent for
any reason, Please limit ALL absences to_those absolutely necessary. At least 80% attendance rate is
required for credit. Parents must contact the school about absences (Title 70-10-106, Section 232) before
10:00 a.m. the day a student is absent, to verify the reason for the absence. Excused absences are school
related functions. Doctors notes will be placed in students file. Doctors notes MUST be original signatures.
It is the responsibility of the student or the student’s parent or guardian to ask teachers for any work
missed due to absence. Students shall have at least as many days as they were absent plus one (1) more
day to make up work. (Teachers may elect to grant more time at their discretion.) Failure to make up work
within the allotted time will result in a grade of “O/F” for each missed assignment/test. Students with
unexcused absences will be expected to turn in assignments on original due date or earn a “0/F”. If
students miss more than 10 days in a semester, it will be the recommendation of Holly Creek School
that the student will not pass that semester.

According to Oklahoma Law, students “must be in attendance two of the first three hours of the school day
to be recorded present for one-half day. (Section 210:10-1-5) Likewise, a student must be in attendance
two of the final three hours to be recorded present for one-half day”. Consequently, students arriving after
9:00 a.m. and leaving before 1:00 p.m. are absent one-half day.

Tardiness: Students are tardy after 8:00 A.M. Tardiness, except in unusual and unavoidable
circumstances, is improper in both business and social affairs. It distracts the teacher, other students, and
causes time lost from the class. Continual tardiness is a habit and reflects upon a person’s character. It
also trains your child to be late for work as an adult. Students who enter the classroom more than five
(5) minutes after the bell has rung after the previous class, except first period, are considered tardy.
Three (3) tardies count as one (1) absence and will be recorded as such in the attendance register. If
tardies continue to be a problem additional disciplinary consequences may result. If a student has repeated
or unjustified tardies, a letter may be sent to inform the parents and the truancy officer of the situation.

Truancy laws are in place to help your student develop good work habits. It is very important that parents
understand the laws about school attendance at school. Parents can be fined if they allow their children to
remain absent from school without a valid excuse. Oklahoma law makes the parent responsible for a
child’s attendance at school. — Parents can be fined up to $100.00 for every day that their child is truant
from school. (Title 70-10-105)

“If a child is absent without valid excuse four (4) or more days or parts of days within a four-week
period or is absent without valid excuse for ten (10) or more days or parts of days within a semester, the
attendance officer shall notify the parent and immediately report such absences to the district attorney for
juvenile proceedings pursuant to Title 10 of the Oklahoma Statutes. (70-10-106, Section 232)” This law
applies to any child between the ages of five and eighteen.

Discipline Policy and Student-Earned Consequences are set forth as follows: The Board of
Education believes that the school’s primary goal is to educate, not discipline. To discipline is to train or
develop self-control. Punishment is the penalty one pays for not having discipline. Parents, we get the
child you have trained. When you do not train your child to be disciplined, his/her education becomes very
difficult. Oklahoma Law, Title 70, Section 6-114, provides teachers as the same rights as parents to control
and discipline school children within local school policies.

When it is necessary to impose discipline, teachers and administrators will follow a progressive discipline
process. The degree of disciplinary actions imposed will be in proportion to the severity of the behavior of
a student and will take into account the student’s discipline history, the age of the student, and other
relevant factors. :

Each student (and his/her parent/guardian) enrolling at Holly Creek School must be willing to accept the
concepts of personal dignity and responsibility through a commitment to scholarly effort, courteous
behavior, and respect for others.

Student Suspension: (Removed from the regular school setting) may be earned if a student is found
guilty of immorality, has been legally determined a delinquent for a nonviolent offense, or violation of
school regulations. Students may be suspended from school when all reasonable options have been
exhausted. The student shall be given oral or written notice of the charge before suspension. If charges are
denied, an explanation of evidence and opportunity to present his/her case will be given. Suspended
students shall have the right to appeal to the board of education. After investigation, the board shall
determine the guilt/innocence of student and reasonableness of the term of suspension. The decision of the
board is final. Students posing a continuing danger may be removed from school immediately. Written
notice and a hearing shall soon follow. Students suspended shall be allowed to complete assignments for a
grade in either a home-based setting, supervised by parent/guardian, or other appropriate setting with
educational monitor as prescribed by school administration. Students must turn-in completed assignments
the day they return to school. Students are barred from campus (including any/all school sponsored
activities) for the length of their suspension.

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) is designed in accordance with Section 438
of the General Education Provisions Act for providing notification of the privacy rights of parents and
students. Records are available for inspection during the regular school day.
1. The parents of Holly Creek School students have the right to inspect and review their child’s education
records.
2. The District’s intent is to limit disclosures of information contained in a student’s education records except:

a) By prior written consent of student's parenUguardian.
b) As directory information
c) Under certain limited circumstances, as permitted by the FERPA.

3. The student’s parent has the right to seek to correct parts of the student's education record that he/she
believes to be inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of student's right. This right includes the right to a
hearing to present evidence that the record should be changed if the district decides not to alter it according to
the parent’s request.

4. Any person has the right to file a complaint with the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare if the
Holly Creek School District violates the FERPA.

5. The student’s parent may obtain a copy of the FERPA policies from the Superintendents office.

6. The school will arrange to provide translations of this notice to non-English speaking parents in their native
language when they become aware that there is a need to do so.

The National Instructional Materials Accessibility Standard (NIMAS) is a new provision in the reauthorization of
the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) 2004 (34 CFR 300.172 and 300.210). The NIMAS is designed
to maximize access to the general education curriculum for blind or other print-disabled children through the timely
provision of accessible instructional materials created from NIMAS source files, a uniform electronic (or digital)
format for textbooks and related materials which is coded in Extensible Markup Language (XML). XML provides a
flexible way to create common information formats and share both the format and the data on the World Wide Web,
intranets, and elsewhere. A glossary of terms is included in the appendix.

Drug-Free School policies are in place for the safety and well being of students and staff. It is the absolute position of
this school district that the use of illicit drugs/alcohol or unlawful possession of drugs/alcohol is wrong and harmful.
Therefore, the following policies and procedures are in place for both students and staff.

1. Alcohol/drug use and abuse education is provided to all students, Pre-Kindergarten through eighth grade.

2. Disciplinary sanctions are in place to address unlawful possession, use, sale, distribution, or being under the
influence of alcohol, illicit drugs, inhalants, or look-alike drugs in school buildings, on school property, at
school-sponsored events, and/or school buses. These disciplinary sanctions also apply to drug abuse
paraphernalia and/or chemicals to prepare, mix, cut, or alter drugs.

3. Information and procedures are also available on alcohol/drug abuse intervention/treatment and the use of
chemical testing for drugs.

4, Procedures are also in place for the use of drug detection animals and equipment in respect to search and
confiscation of alcohol, drugs, or other contraband.

5. Student offenders of this policy can expect suspension and may be required to also obtain a chemical abuse
assessment, drug free chemical test, and verification of completion of a treatment at their personal expense.
In the event of arrest, parents/ guardians will be notified concurrent with legal action.

6. Good faith cooperation with all alcohol/drug policy and procedures is required or students will be expelled.

Search and Seizure Policies provide that students in the Holly Creek School System will have the privilege of being
assigned a desk and/or lockers that shall remain the property of the school district. Pupils shall have no reasonable
expectation of privacy toward school officials regarding any and all desks/lockers or other school property assigned to
them. (Oklahoma School Statutes section 24-102, Title 70 of Section 489). All shall be subject to inspection and
searched by administrators and/or teachers as deemed necessary upon suspicion that stolen articles, contraband,
alcoholic beverages, or controlled dangerous substances may be found therein.

Any student found to have stolen articles, contraband, alcoholic beverages, or controlled dangerous substances in
his/her possession shall be subject to disciplinary action and his/her parents shall be notified of such action. However,
if such acts of students are contrary to criminal law, then the student will be referred to the parents and proper law
officials.

Procedures to Inspect Education Records are as follows: The parent of a student or an eligible student may inspect
the student’s education records upon request. In some circumstances, it may be mutually more convenient for the
record custodian to provide copies of records.
Since a student's records may be maintained in several locations, the school administrator will offer to collect copies of
records or the records themselves, so they may be inspected at one site. However, if a parent or eligible student wishes
to inspect records where they are maintained, school administrators will make every effort to accommodate their
wishes,

The parent or eligible student should submit to the student’s school administrator a written request which identifies, as
precisely as possible, the record or records he or she wishes to inspect.

The administrator (or other record custodian) will make the needed arrangements as promptly as possible and notify the
parent or eligible student of the time and place where the records may be inspected. This procedure must be completed
in 45 days or less from the receipt of the request for access.

If, for any valid reason, such as working hours, distance between record location sites, or health, a parent or eligible
student cannot personally inspect and review a student’s educational record, the school district will arrange for the
parent or eligible student to obtain copies of the record.

Meningitis: Is a common name for infections that take place in the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord.
Meningitis can be caused by viruses and by bacteria. A person with meningitis needs to be seen by a health-care
provider immediately.

If you have any questions concerning meningitis, please call the school to speak with an administrator or call the local
health department.
