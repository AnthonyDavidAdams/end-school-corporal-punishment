# Texas school risk pools and liability programs

Research for the Safe Schools Project (EarthPilot). Purpose: identify the people who run the pools that insure Texas public school districts, so we can talk with them about corporal punishment claims.

Compiled 2026-10-13. Every name, title, email and phone below was read on the cited page on that date. Nothing is guessed. Where a page gave no email, none is listed. Read-only research; no one has been contacted.

---

## 1. TASB Risk Management Fund (the big one)

- **Website:** https://www.tasbrmf.org/
- **Administered by:** Texas Association of School Boards, Inc. (TASB), Austin. The Fund is a self-insured, not-for-profit interlocal risk pool, not an insurance company.
- **Members:** "more than 1,000 Fund members in Texas" (governance page, https://www.tasbrmf.org/about/governance). The Fund's own history article says "approximately 1,000 school districts, education service centers, and community colleges" (https://www.tasbrmf.org/resources/insights/strength-through-collaboration). TASA's partner listing quotes the Fund: "Started in 1974, the TASB Risk Management Fund is Texas' largest provider of risk management services to educational entities, but we are not an insurance company. We are a self-insured, not-for-profit risk pool of more than 1,000 Texas public education organizations" (https://tasanet.org/partnerships/corporate-partners/).
- **What it covers:** Auto, Cybersecurity, Liability, Property, Unemployment Compensation, Violent Act, Workers' Compensation.
- **Liability coverage and legal defense** (https://www.tasbrmf.org/coverages/liability):
  - "If the business of education moves from the classroom to the courtroom, we deliver expert legal support and litigation management services."
  - "Our panel of attorneys is trained to defend members in legal issues unique to school districts."
  - "The Fund's School Liability coverage combines General Liability and Professional Legal Liability into a single coverage agreement to eliminate coverage gaps"
  - "Professional Legal Liability protects your schools, employees, board members, and volunteers against allegations that they harmed someone while doing their jobs. Examples include employment discrimination, constitutional rights violations, and sexual harassment."
  - Also "law enforcement liability, employee benefits liability, and wrongful acts coverage."
  - Fund history article: "The Texas Tort Claims Act (TTCA) limits the liability of political subdivisions, including school districts, in employee negligence claims." and the Fund's staff and legal team are "trained to handle and defend claims that fall under the TTCA." (https://www.tasbrmf.org/resources/insights/strength-through-collaboration)

## 2. Texas Political Subdivisions Joint Self-Insurance Fund (TPS)

- **Website:** https://tpspool.org (branded "Texas Political Subdivision JSIF"; "Serving Texas Since 1983")
- **Address / phone:** 14990 Landmark Blvd, Dallas, TX 75254; (800) 588-0013 (https://tpspool.org/contact-tps/). No contact form, no individual emails anywhere on the site.
- **Members:** Count not published. Serves "Texas municipalities, counties, school districts, hospitals, junior colleges, and other political subdivisions" (https://tpspool.org/news/tps-celebrates-40-years-of-growth/). TASA's partner page carries TPS's blurb "hundreds of school districts, cities, counties, and public entities participate in the Pool" (https://tasanet.org/partnerships/corporate-partners/).
- **What it covers:** Auto, Crime, Cyber, Crisis Management/Active Assailant, General Liability, Law Enforcement Liability, Public Officials Liability, School Board Liability, Property, Workers' Compensation, and a "Small Schools Program" for coastal/hail-zone districts (https://tpspool.org/coverage/small-schools-program).
- **School Board Legal Liability** (https://tpspool.org/coverage/school-board-liability/): covers "alleged errors and omissions in the performance of duties by elected or appointed board members and employees"; "Defense Expenses Outside Limits of Liability".

### Corporal punishment: TPS names it as a covered peril
https://tpspool.org/coverage/general-liability/ (read in full in Chrome, 2026-10-13), under "Comprehensive Coverage Features":

> "Corporal Punishment Coverage (for Schools): Provides coverage for claims related to corporal punishment incidents within educational institutions."

Same list: "Defense Expenses Outside Limits of Liability: Covers defense expenses separately from the policy limits, preserving your coverage for settlements or judgments." and "Rendering or Failure to Render Aid Coverage (for Schools/Colleges)". The page also says "we offer separate coverage documents for schools/colleges and public entities (non-school)."

This is the only Texas pool page found that mentions corporal punishment by name. No loss-control bulletin or training on it was found on tpspool.org (news index https://tpspool.org/news/ is boilers, cyber, bus lines, safety grants).

## 3. Texas Association of Public Schools Property and Liability Fund (TAPS) — DEFUNCT

- TAPS was a San Antonio-based pool for school districts and community colleges. It filed for bankruptcy in October 2017.
  - Pew Charitable Trusts, Feb 6, 2018: "only one—the Texas Association of Public Schools Property and Liability Fund, in October—has filed for bankruptcy in the last 16 years." (https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/fact-sheets/2018/02/local-governments-rarely-file-for-bankruptcy)
  - MySA, Oct 19, 2017, "San Antonio insurer for schools goes bankrupt": "The Texas Association of Public Schools Property and Liability Fund, also known as TAPS, sought protection in U.S. Bankruptcy Court in San..." (Google snippet; article at mysanantonio.com now redirects to an unrelated story). Case no. 5:17-bk-52437 (W.D. Tex.).
  - Robert V. Reim, "Risk Pools and Texas School Districts" (Aug 2019): "In 2017, TAPS filed bankruptcy, leaving claims unpaid and requiring member assessments." and "Too high of Hail claim retention forced TAPS into bankruptcy." (https://www.robertreim.com/wp-content/uploads/Risk-Pools-RVR-2019.pdf)
- No website exists (tapsfund.org does not resolve). Post-bankruptcy litigation continued into 2025 (CourtListener docket, TAPS v. White Deer ISD, 5:18-cv-00985; Pharr-San Juan-Alamo ISD v. TAPS, Tex. Sup. Ct. 2022). Not a live target for outreach.

## 5. Public Educators Association of Texas (PEAT)

- **Website:** https://www.peat-tx.com
- **What it is:** "The Public Educators Association of Texas (PEAT) is a property and casualty fund formed by the Texas Interlocal Cooperation Act to provide coverages for Texas educational institutions." Established Sept 1, 2005 under Gov't Code ch. 791 (https://www.peat-tx.com/about-us). Fully reinsured ("purchases reinsurance that completely covers potential claims").
- **Covers:** Property, Auto, General Liability, Excess Liability, Crime, Equipment Breakdown, Educators Professional Liability, Law Enforcement, Guardians & Marshals (home page).
- **Administered by:** "The Public Educators Association of Texas (PEAT) program is administered by Public Risk Underwriters of Texas (PRU), which has overseen the program since its establishment in 2005." (https://www.peat-tx.com/about/administration/). Claims: "Claims service for the Public Educators Association of Texas program is managed by Claims Administrative Services (CAS), based in Tyler, Texas." (https://www.peat-tx.com/claims/)
- **Members:** count not published. Reim 2019 table lists PEAT member equity at $114,168 (2017).

## Summary of what was and was not found

Found: leadership and governance for TASB RMF (Mary Barrett; chair Anthony Hill, Coppell ISD; ~1,000 members), TPS (Scott Payne; chair Gary Earnest; corporal punishment named as a covered GL peril), PCAT (board of five ISD officials, 160+ districts, INSURICA-run), PEAT (Gary Howell; chair Josh Johnson, Carlisle ISD; PRU/CAS staff with direct emails), RMC of Texas (Shaun Barnett, Cari Ledesma with direct emails), TEE (no people named). TAPS is bankrupt since Oct 2017.

Not found: any published email for TASB RMF or TPS executives, counsel or claims directors (only switchboards and TASB's regional risk-solutions consultants); TPS and PEAT member counts; a general counsel at any pool except TASB's Paul Taylor (Director of Legal and Regulatory Affairs); any loss-control bulletin, model policy, or training on corporal punishment, student discipline, or excessive force at any pool. The only text that names corporal punishment is TPS's general-liability feature list quoted in section 2.
