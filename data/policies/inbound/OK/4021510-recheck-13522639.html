U.S. Government

Course Syllabus

Course Overview:

If there is ever a need to contact me I can be best reached at my email ([email protected])       during the day. I check it periodically throughout the day and in a normal scenario will get back to you in a timely manner. If I for whatever reason can’t be reached at my email, my room number is 405-387-4304 ext. 6426. The best times to reach me would be during my planning period which is from 9:55 – 10:45. Help will be available for all students before school Monday through Thursday when asked and set up before. If this time doesn’t work for the student, the student needs to come to me and make prior arraignments for extra help.

 

Course Content:

          This course is a general survey course of the Government in the U.S.  This course will be geared around primary documents, lectures, critical thinking and analysis, as well as classroom discussions and debates.  This course aims at preparing the students for college by teaching them effective note-taking techniques, as well as the ability to organize information from lectures to construct a timeline of relevant events in American history.  The second semester concludes with a study of the U.S Economics. 

 

Course Materials:

          Every day you need to bring these items to class:

                                    -Pen or pencil

                                    -A 3 ring binder and a note book over 1 inch in size

                                    In the binder needs to be Bell Work (You will be given 7 minutes to complete this task every day and no longer than 7 minutes.) and all the units from the section.

         

Course Policies:

1.   Be Respectful to everyone in the classroom (teacher, students, subs, etc…)

      I will not tolerate cutting each other down.

All violations of the student handbook will also be enforced.

                                    1st violation: Verbal warning

                  2nd violation: Private talk and consultation with parents

                  3rd violation: After school detention

                  4th violation: Referral to the office

Expectations:

-       Come to class on time.

-       Do your work.

-       Be prepared to learn.

-       Follow the rule.

 

Cell Phones:

          Any cell phone heard or seen in class will be taken and given to the administration. Texting in your pocket will also warrant losing your phone. Your book bags need zipped after the material from class is taken out. I don’t want you to think you can text while putting your hand in your bag.

 

Hall Pass:

            You will be allowed three hall passes a semester. Understand that you still can only leave when notes are not being taken. Going to the restroom is not something to be done during class. That is to be done in between classes. If this becomes a problem then no one in the class will be allowed to leave for any reason other than emergency. An “emergency” bathroom brake does not fall under an emergency.

 

Sentences:

            “All students will be required to complete one assignment each week that involves writing.  Often times, this assignment will be also tied to a reading assignment, and this assignment will be graded and recorded.  All students will be expected to write in complete sentences.  Beginning September 10th, written responses not written in complete sentence form will receive a point deduction equivalent to 50% of the point value for that particular question. For example, if a question is worth 10 points and the student writes the correct answer, but it is not in complete sentence form, the student will lose 5 points on that particular question.

 

 Beginning on the first day of school, and continuing through September 7th, students who receive a 50% reduction on any particular question for not answering in complete sentence form will have their graded paper returned to them and be given two days to change the answers into complete sentence form.  If a student follows this procedure, any point reductions that were a result of incomplete sentences will be removed.  It is important to note this opportunity is being offered as a transition time for students to meet our expectation of communicating and writing in complete sentences.  Beginning September 10th, students will receive a 50% point reduction for each item not answered in complete sentence form, without the opportunity to redo the assignment.”

 

 

Grading Scale:

          This class will be a system of total points. Each assignment will be given a point total. The total accumulation of points will be divided out of the maximum amount of points and that will be your grade. Therefore all the points are equal in the classroom. For example a question worth 5 points on a test and 5 points on a daily work assignment are equal. Traditional homework will be between 15 and 25 points and on average the tests have 85-100 points. 80% of your grade will be from the entire semester and the last 20% will come from the final.

 

Late Work:

          Late work will be accepted but each day of the assignment being late will carry a penalty.

            1 Day Late- 40% deducted

            2 Days Late- 60% deducted

            3 Days Late- 80% deducted

After 3 days the assignment will not be allowed to be turned in. The student handbook will be followed in cases of late work following an absence. At the end of the semester I will drop ONE and only one homework assignment.

 

Cheating:

 

          Anyone caught cheating will be given a zero on an assignment the assignment before and after. I want to know, what you know, not your neighbor and his or her name will be turned into the administration.

            Examples of cheating:

-       Letting someone copy your homework (Both parties will be given a zero.)

-       Looking at someone’s paper during a test.

-       Texting 

Plagiarism:

“All writing you do must be your own work or properly cited.  The proper way to cite someone else’s work is contained in Writers Inc.  If the work is not your own or properly cited, you are guilty of plagiarism.  The following applies to plagiarism:

1. Word-for-word copying, without acknowledgement, of the language of another writer.  Having someone else write or dictate all or part of your work for you is plagiarism of this kind.  In addition, you should copy NO printed passage, no matter how brief, without acknowledging the source and either placing it in quotation marks or setting it inside a block quote.  This applies to even the briefest of passages if they are truly distinctive.

2.  The unacknowledged paraphrasing of an author’s ideas.  You should no more take credit for another’s thoughts than you should for another’s written words.  Any distinctively original idea taken from another writer should be credited to its author.  If you are not sure whether an author’s idea is distinctive or not, assume that it is and cite the work or ask me for advice.

3.  Plagiarism applies not only to the written word but also to material on the Internet, CD-ROMs, computer disks, and other electronic media.”

(The above section on plagiarism was taken from a plagiarism statement written by Richard Mueller, which was adapted from the writings of Dr. Pamela Pittman of the University of Central Oklahoma.)

 

PLEASE NOTE:  The penalty for plagiarism (as a minimum) is a zero grade on that particular assignment.  If you are unsure of any citation, please ask your teacher. Refer to your handbook for potential additional penalties.
