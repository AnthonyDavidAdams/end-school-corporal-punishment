MISSION STATEMENT

Believing that all students can learn; Bristow Public Schools shares with our parents,
our students, and our community the responsibility of educating our youth to assure that
all students make a smooth transition from school to life.
PARENTS’ BILL OF RIGHTS

The school district is in compliance with the Parents’ Bill of Rights. Additional
information is available for parents in the school policy manual on this subject. Parents
may submit written requests to obtain the specific information listed in the Parents’ Bill
of Rights law during regular school business hours by contacting the building principal
or the superintendent. 25 O.S. Section 2001

RESPONSIBILITY OF PARENTS

1. Support the schools in requiring students to observe all school rules and regulations
and accepting responsibility for any willful misbehavior on their part. Send students
to school after proper attention has been given to their health, personal cleanliness,
and appropriate dress.

2. Maintain an active interest in the student's work. Make it possible for them to
complete assigned homework particularly by providing a quiet place and conditions
for study.

3. Comply with the school’s requests. This includes reading carefully all
communications and signing and returning them as requested.

4. Ensure that your children attend school regularly without unnecessary absences and
arrive punctually at school daily.

5. Collaborate with the school by attending conferences that encompass the planning
of continuous maintenance and school improvement.

RESPONSIBILITY OF STUDENT

GENERAL CONDUCT: Students will be seated and working on assignments when
class starts. Students will respond properly to adults. Students will respond to the
teacher's raised hand to gain class attention. Students will not be dismissed until the
teacher dismisses them. Students will follow the six pillars of character.

QUALITY OF WORK: Students shall do their best each day and complete assigned
lessons including homework and assignments missed due to absences.

SCHOOL RULES: Each student shall conform to school rules and regulations and
respect the leadership of teachers, school officials, and others who have been
assigned leadership responsibility.

CARE OF SCHOOL PROPERTY: A modern, fully-equipped school building
designed for beauty and utility is part of the heritage of the Bristow High School
student body. It is the privilege and obligation of every student to carefully use,
preserve, and pass the building, its grounds, and its equipment without blemish to
future generations. Anyone who damages or defaces any school property shall
make restitution for the property that has been damaged.

Grade Point Average

Beginning in the ninth grade year, all students maintain a grade point average (GPA)
which is part of his/her permanent record. Each student's GPA is based upon semester
grades received in each class and is calculated upon completion of each semester that
the student is in attendance. The GPA is used to determine student eligibility into such
groups as National Honor Society and other school organizations. In addition, the
student's GPA is used as one of the criteria for holding class or student council office.
GPA is also used to determine each student's rank in class. GPA and class rank are
often used as criteria upon which a student's college admission is based. Students may
check their cumulative GPA/class rank at any time through the counseling office or the
registrar’s office.

Class Rank

All students will be ranked using a “simple rank” method. Ranking will be determined at
the end of each semester and all subjects will be included.

through 12. The test will account for a maximum of up to 20% of the semester grade

has the opportunity to qualify for exemption from taking their semester tests. In an effort
to raise a final grade, any student may take their semester tests even though he/she
may be exempt, and it cannot be used to the student’s detriment.

Valedictorian & Salutatorian Policy

Valedictorian and salutatorian honors will be determined based on the highest grade
point average calculated at the completion of the seventh semester. In the event of a tie,
all students with the highest GPA will share the valedictorian honor. Salutatorian honors
will be awarded to the student(s) with the next highest GPA. Grade point averages will
be calculated to four (4) decimal places with no rounding.

To be eligible for valedictorian or salutatorian honors, a student must have attended
Bristow High School for at least one full year. GPA calculations will include final grades
earned from ninth grade through the first semester of twelfth grade.

Bristow High School uses a 5.0 weighted grading scale to encourage students to pursue
advanced academic coursework. Weighted courses include Advanced Placement (AP),
honors, and concurrent college courses. These courses provide students with
opportunities for rigorous study and, in some cases, college credit.

For GPA purposes, grades earned in AP, honors, and concurrent enrollment courses
are weighted as follows:

ATTENDANCE REQUIREMENTS

Good attendance is of prime importance for the educational development of each
student. A student should never be absent unless it is absolutely necessary. With regard
to unexcused absences, a student commits a criminal offense if the student is absent
for three days or parts of days during a four week period or eight days or parts of days
during a semester. The state compulsory attendance law allows for legal action, if
necessary, for excessive absences. Violations will result in the parents or guardians
being referred to the District Attorney for prosecution under the current Oklahoma
Statute.

In order to receive credit, a student must miss no more than ten (10) days in a
semester. When a student reaches their third absence, they will be notified, and a letter
will be sent home to the parents. If a student exceeds ten absences, he/she may appeal
to the Attendance Committee. A student who is absent more than ten days during the
semester may not be given credit except as noted below:

Any student who is absent more than ten days during the semester will have his/her

attendance record reviewed by the campus Attendance Committee to determine if

extenuating circumstances exist. Extenuating circumstances include, but are not limited

to, the following:

1. Illness or death in the immediate family

2. Late enrollment or early withdrawal of a student placed in or having resided in a
community care home

3. Late enrollment or early withdrawal of a migratory student as defined by applicable
federal regulations

4. The observance of religious holy days if the parent submits a written request

5. Weather or road conditions making travel dangerous

6. A natural disaster

*It is not necessary for a student to have a note from a docior each time he/she is ill.

However, if the number of absences exceeds ten days in a semester, and if medical

reasons are given for an additional absence, a doctor's note will be required for the

day(s) of illness in order to receive academic credit unless waived by the Attendance

Committee. Students who attend classes for part of the day and leave school for a

medical or dental appointment may have that absence excused in any classes missed if

Students who are not in attendance for more than ten minutes of a class will be
considered absent for that class. This absence will count as an unexcused absence and

Tardy Policy

Definition of Tardy: A tardy is entry to class anytime after the tardy bell rings. Any
student who has been detained by the office or by a teacher must obtain a “no tardy”
slip from the person detaining the student. No penalty is placed on the student for this
type of tardy. A tardy is not excused by having the parent sign the student in, nor is a
tardy excused when the parent is the cause of the tardy.

Consequences for Tardiness: Students may have two cumulative tardies per class per
nine weeks without penalty. On the sixth tardy, one day of absence will be accessed
with a day of absence assessed for every third tardy thereafter. All additional tardies

may be penalized as follows:

Tardy 3-5 may result in one day of noon detention for each tardy.

Tardy 6-8 may result in one day of Saturday detention for each tardy.

Tardy 9-11 may result in a day of OCD for each tardy, or alternative punishment at the
Principal's discretion.

Tardy in excess of 11 may result in one day of off-campus suspension for each tardy. It
is important to be on time as it is to be in attendance. In most jobs, persons who arrive
late are penalized. BHS has developed rules and penalties in an effort to help students
learn the habit of promptness. The attendance coordinator will keep a master file of
tardiness in the attendance office. Tardies are cumulative each semester.

Student Absentee Procedures

Students are required to sign-in at the attendance office upon arriving at school after the

Medication Policy

Bristow High School personnel are not permitted to give medication of any kind,
including analgesics, similar preparations, or any other drugs, unless the parent
requests in writing that there is a need for such medication and the parent provides the
medication. If the circumstances are questionable, the school employee reserves
the right to deny the parent's request. Students will be scheduled to come to the
school nurse or of the Principal’s designee for medicines.

When administering prescription medicines, the school district would prefer to have a
written statement from a physician or dentist licensed to practice in the United States.
Information, however, placed on a prescription label, if it is precise and clear to the
school nurse or of the principal’s designee, may be substituted for the above-noted
statement. The prescription must be filled by a pharmacist licensed to practice in the
United States. If prescription medicine is required, it must be in its original container,
kept in locked storage in the office of the school nurse or of the principal’s designee,
and administered by the nursing staff or a school employee. The school may accept a
sufficient quantity of medicine for one month. Parents or guardians must bring all
prescription medication to a location designated by the principal. Upon receipt, the
medicine will be inventoried and discrepancies reported to parents. Only prescription
medication needed for the student to remain in school will be administered.
22
Non-prescription medications will only be administered for a period of two weeks with
written parental permission. A physician's note will be required for any non-prescription
medication needed for longer than two weeks.

The above is not intended to prevent a student from having in his or her possession and
taking analgesics, allergy tablets, or other similar nonprescription medication during the
school day. The medication must be in its original container. It is strongly recommended
that the student go to the nurse's office or a location designated by the Principal since
the consumption of pills in any form during the school day or on the school campus
increases the likelihood of a student being mistakenly charged with a violation of the
district's policy on drug abuse. Medication with limited duration will be administered for
the time prescribed on the label, and any leftover medicine not picked up by the parent
will be destroyed after two weeks.

Forms for parental authorization of administration of medicines are available in the
Office of the principal. Both prescription and over-the-counter medicines will be
administered by the nursing staff or a school employee only with parent authorization,
pursuant to school policy. Medication must be taken to the registrar’s office where it will
be securely kept until dosage time.

DISCIPLINE

When a student within the system fails to abide by the code of conduct, it becomes the
jurisdiction and responsibility of the school to administer appropriate discipline so that
the student(s) may continue their education. This is particularly important since we wish
our students to learn that real-life situations require a code of conduct. When an
individual(s) fails to conform to that code of conduct, immediate disciplinary
consequences can be expected.

The responsibility of the school is to teach its students what is acceptable behavior. The
outcome of this instruction is to teach the student self-restraint and self-discipline, which
are applicable to success in the real world. This instruction extends to the classroom,
building, grounds, school activities, and school events. Every school staff member is
charged with instructing students about what an acceptable code of conduct is for a
given situation or circumstance.

For practicality, offenses that require disciplinary actions should be classified into major
and minor offenses. Generally, minor offenses are those that occur and can be dealt
with and resolved immediately on the site by a staff member. Minor offenses include, but
are not limited to, tardies, failure to work on assignments, talking without permission,
dress code violations, public displays of affection, behaviors distracting from the
learning environment, and disrespect to other students, staff, and/or patrons.

30
Major offenses are those that threaten the safety of the students, staff, administration,
and learning environment. Students who challenge the authority of the school to
execute its function of educating students will not be tolerated and will suffer the
appropriate consequences. Such offenses include, but are not limited to, repeated
minor offenses; truancy; theft; vandalism; physical or verbal abuse of students, faculty,
or patrons; extortion; blackmail; coercion; hazing; possession/control or under the
influence of alcohol, drugs, and/or dangerous substances; possession of dangerous
weapons; improper, unruly, violent behavior, immoral acts; indecent exposure;
provoking and/or aiding a fight, notes threatening bodily harm, vocalizing bodily harm;
vile language; tobacco in any form; trespassing; gambling; or non-intoxicating
beverages (as defined by state law). Major offenses require immediate administrative
action and notification of the parents.

Punishment

Punishment here is defined as action taken beyond a verbal reprimand that requires
the student to perform an action of retribution outside the normal classroom instructional
activities. The school will notify a parent when the child’s behavior is not changing after
normal school punishment.

The school authorizes the teacher to employ punishment for the minor offenses she/he
has clearly defined as inappropriate behavior. The punishment should be appropriate for
the severity of the offense and for the inappropriate behavior. Because the system

views punishment as a teaching strategy, the punishment should be issued and
monitored by the teacher in a concerned, non-threatening manner. Teachers are
authorized to use, but are not limited to, the following punishment strategies: student
conference; time-out; structured detention before school, during noon hour, or after
school; classroom service work; Friday detention; referral to counselor; parental contact;
and/or referral to office. When the punishment has been assigned, the teacher will
inform the parent of the student’s behavior and the action taken.

The administration is authorized to handle major offenses. These offenses are
considered to be threatening to the overall atmosphere of the school and require more
severe punishment strategies. Results include, but are not limited to, detention, Friday
school, corporal punishment, counseling, school service work, on-campus-detention,
out-of-school suspension, ineligibility for attendance or participation in school activities,
and long-term suspension.

Suspension

Students who violate the student code of conduct may be suspended by the principal or
designee. Suspensions shall not extend beyond the current school semester and the
succeeding semester. Students who have been suspended have the right to appeal the
decision to the Site Suspension Review Committee if the suspension is ten days or less.
If a parent wishes to appeal the suspension, they will have five school days to notify, in
writing, the Principal or Assistant Principal of their desire to appeal. If the suspension is

31
more than ten days, the decision may be appealed to the Superintendent of Schools.

A student who has been off-campus suspended will be given an opportunity to request
assignments* from his/her teachers during the suspension period and, upon returning to
school, will have one day to complete and turn in all assignments.

On-Campus Detention

On Campus Detention (OCD) shall be utilized for the infractions of rules where, in the
opinion of the administration, the interest of the school and of the students will be
served. Location of OCD will be in a portable building on the high school campus.

Students will be monitored by a hired staff member and will do assignments sent by
his/her teachers for full credit. OCD will only be provided if funding is available.

NOTICE OF RIGHTS UNDER FERPA AND NOTICE FOR DIRECTORY
INFORMATION

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), a federal law, affords parents
and students over 18 years of age (“eligible students”) certain rights with respect to the
student’s education records. They are:

1. The right to inspect and review the student's education records within 45 days of the
day the district receives a request for access.

Parents or eligible students should submit to the school principal (or appropriate school
official) a written request that identifies the record(s) they wish to inspect. The
principal/school official will make arrangements for access and notify the parent or
eligible student of the time and place where the records may be inspected.

2. The right to request the amendment of the student's education records that the
parent or eligible student believes are inaccurate or misleading.

Parents or eligible students may ask the school district to amend a record believed to be
inaccurate or misleading. They should write to the school principal or appropriate school
official, clearly identify the part of the record they want changed, and specify why it is
inaccurate or misleading.

34
If the district decides not to amend the record as requested by a parent or eligible
student, the district will notify the parent or eligible student of the decision and advise
him/her of the right to a hearing regarding the request for amendment.

3. The right to consent to disclosures of personally identifiable information contained in
the student's education records, except to the extent that FERPA authorizes disclosure
without consent.

One exception that permits disclosure without consent is disclosure to school officials
with legitimate educational interests. A school official is a person employed by the
district as an administrator, supervisor, instructor, or support staff member (including
health or medical staff and law enforcement unit personnel); a person serving on the
school board; a person or company with whom the district has contracted to perform a
special task (such as an attorney, auditor, medical consultant, or therapist); or a parent
or student participating in a school service program or serving on an official committee,
such as a disciplinary or grievance committee, or assisting another school official in
performing his or her tasks. A school official has a legitimate educational interest if the
Official needs to review an education record in order to fulfill his or professional
responsibility.

Upon request, the district may disclose education records without consent to officials of
another school district in which a student seeks or intends to enroll.

4. The right to file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education concerning
alleged failures by the district to comply with the requirements of FERPA.

5. FERPA also requires that Bristow Public Schools, with certain exceptions, obtain
your written consent prior to the disclosure of personally identifiable information from
your child's education records. However, the district may disclose appropriately
designated “directory information” without written consent, unless you have advised the
district to the contrary in accordance with district procedures. The primary purpose of
directory information is to allow the district to include this type of information from your
child’s education records in certain school publications. Examples include:

A playbill, showing your student's role in a drama production;

Graduation programs; and

Sports activity sheets, such as for wrestling, showing weight and height of team
members.

If you do not want Bristow Public Schools to disclose directory information from your
child's education records without your prior written consent, you must notify the district
in writing within two weeks of receiving this notice. The district has designated the
following information as directory information, which may be released without obtaining
parental consent:

e Student's name

grade, sophomore)

Student's participation in extracurricular activities

Student’s achievements, awards, and honors

Weight and height of members of athletic teams

6. Directory information, which is information that is generally not considered harmful or
an invasion of privacy if released, can also be disclosed to outside organizations without
a parent's prior written consent. Outside organizations include, but are not limited to,
companies that manufacture class rings or publish yearbooks. In addition, two federal
laws require local educational agencies receiving assistance under the Elementary and
Secondary Education Act of 1965 (ESEA) to provide military recruiters, upon request,
with three directory information categories — names, addresses, and telephone listings —
unless parents have advised the district that they do not want their student's information
