# The letter to families

Sent to every family, in the family's language, in the same week staff are told and before students
are. Printed and mailed, not only posted; the families most affected are the least likely to be on
the app. The superintendent signs it, not a principal, because the district is the one acknowledging.

Placeholders in [BRACKETS]. Keep it to one page. The facts are from `../facts/claims/`.

> **[DISTRICT NAME] School District**
> **[DATE]**
>
> Dear families,
>
> On [DATE] our Board of Education voted to end corporal punishment — paddling — in all of our
> schools. The change takes effect on [DATE]. I am writing to tell you what is changing, why, and
> what will happen instead.
>
> **What we are acknowledging.** For many years this district has used paddling as a form of
> discipline. Last year we reported to the federal government that [N] of our students received it.
> Some of you received it yourselves as students here. The Board and I have concluded that it was a
> mistake, and we are saying so plainly: no child in this district should have been hit, and no
> child will be again. We are sorry.
>
> **Why now.** The evidence has settled. Students who were paddled in school do worse, on average,
> in school and after it. The American Academy of Pediatrics asked every state to end the practice
> in 2023. Every school district in Kentucky and North Carolina has already ended it. It is
> prohibited in schools in 137 of the 199 countries that are counted. We are not early. We are
> choosing not to be last.
>
> **What happens instead.** Discipline is not going away; the paddle is. Every school will use the
> same published set of responses when a student breaks a rule, from a five-minute reset and a note
> home, to a call you will get from your child in the office with the principal beside them, to a
> meeting with you the same day for anything serious. Every response ends with the student back in
> class and making right whatever they did. The full list is in the student handbook and on the
> wall of every classroom. Suspension is the last step, for safety only, not the second step.
>
> **Every teacher and administrator is being trained** in these responses before the change takes
> effect. That training is why there are ninety days between the vote and the day the policy
> begins.
>
> **You no longer need to opt out.** In the past, some families filed a written objection to keep
> their child from being paddled. That form is no longer needed and will not be offered. No one
> hits your child here, with or without permission. If you had wanted to keep paddling as an
> option, I understand that it comes from wanting your child to behave and to be safe, and I would
> like to talk with you about how we will do that; my number is below.
>
> **What you can do.** Read the handbook page with your child. Tell us when something is not
> working. We will publish, every quarter, how many students were sent to the office, what happened,
> and how many were suspended, so that you can see whether this is working as well as we can.
>
> Thank you for trusting us with your children. We intend to be worthy of it.
>
> [NAME], Superintendent
> [PHONE] · [EMAIL]

## Notes

- The paragraph beginning "If you had wanted to keep paddling" is the one that matters most and the
  one districts most want to cut. It is the difference between a family that feels overruled and a
  family that feels invited. The conversation it offers is in `../training/modules/07-parents.md`
  ("Script: the parent who wanted paddling").
- Attach the office response ladder as the second page, in plain language.
- Send it in every language spoken by more than a handful of families; the district's English-learner
  count is in its own data.
