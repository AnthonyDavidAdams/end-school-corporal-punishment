Each bullet is one account. Quotes are exactly as printed at the URL given; bracketed text is ours. "Permitting state" means the state allowed school corporal punishment at the time of the incident. North Carolina's last districts ended it in 2018; the two NC accounts predate that.

## Former students, recalling it as adults

- **Greg Hooper**, now 31, paddled at 16 at Alvarado High School, Alvarado ISD, Texas, "over a decade ago," for using audio-editing software after finishing his work. Offered in-school suspension or three swats; chose the swats; school phoned his parents first. Verbatim: "He asked me to essentially bend over his desk, and he hit me three times," Hooper told KERA News. "It was humiliating. It was so uncomfortable." And: "He didn't hold back," Hooper said. "It was three full swing hits, and each one hurt more than the last." Source: KERA News, Dylan Duke, 2026-10-12, read at the HPPR republication (URL above). status: verified. Note: KERA's own URL for this story was not located; the HPPR page carries the KERA byline and copyright.

- **Trey Clayton**, 19 at publication, paddled as an 8th grader in March 2011 at Independence High School, Coldwater, Tate County School District, Mississippi, for talking back to a teacher and a librarian; fainted leaving the office after three strikes, breaking his jaw and gashing his chin (court documents). Verbatim: "I'm not going to lie, I was in a lot of trouble during school," Clayton said. "Every time, they gave me the option to get a paddling or get sent home, and I took the paddling." And: "I would go in, get a paddling, come back, go about my day." His mother Dana Hamilton: "When I went to pick him up, my son was spitting teeth into the trash can," she said. Source: Education Week, Sarah D. Sparks, 2016-08-23 (print 2016-08-24). status: verified.

- **Sherrell McGee** (woman), parent and former student, Collins, Covington County School District, Mississippi; paddled as a child and as a high schooler. Verbatim: "I was paddled with a dress on. A dress," she remembers. "Having to bend over, touch the desk, with a dress on. No." Source: The Hechinger Report, Tara García Mathewson, 2022-06-06. status: verified.

- **Damion Rankin Gooden**, 18, paddled in first and second grade at Collins Elementary School, Covington County School District, Mississippi. Verbatim: "You got colors," he explained, "green, yellow, red. Once you get red, you get paddled." Asked whether he was really paddled: "All the time," he said. Source: The Hechinger Report, Tara García Mathewson, 2022-06-06. status: verified.

- **Allison Collins** (girl), senior at Robbinsville High School, Graham County Schools, North Carolina; paddled as a sophomore, her first time in trouble; her father chose the paddle. Verbatim: "That's like my first time ever being in trouble!" And: "And my dad was like, 'Just paddle her.' Because down here in the mountains, we do it the old-school way," Source: WUNC, Jess Clark, 2017-02-23. status: verified. NC permitted at the time; Graham County ended the practice in 2018.

- **Ted Gup**, paddled at 7 in first grade, Belle Stone Elementary School, Canton, Ohio, 1956. Not a permitting state today; included for the sensory detail. Verbatim: "In her strong hands, Mrs. P held a 3-foot-long oak paddle with Greek letters on it — a memento of a college fraternity." "I lurched forward into the wall, stiffening my arms to absorb the shock." "The crack of the paddle resounded down the hall." "I remember limping home over the hill" ... "There were red welts on both cheeks." Source: Salon, Ted Gup (first-person essay), 2001-09-04. status: verified.

## Students interviewed at the time (pseudonyms assigned by HRW/ACLU, 2008)

- **Shannon Q.** (girl), 18, Midland, Texas, interviewed 2008-02-25. Verbatim: "I would have made a different decision if I had known how much it would hurt." Source: HRW/ACLU, A Violent Education, section III, 2008-08. status: verified.

- **LaShell M.** (girl), high school, Mississippi Delta, interviewed 2007-12-04. Verbatim: "He grabs pants to make them tighter on your behind. It hurts more then." Source: same HRW report. status: verified.

- **Matthew S.**, middle school student, Mississippi Delta, interviewed 2007-12-12, describing a paddling in second grade. Verbatim: "It hurt for about two hours, it felt like fire under my butt." Source: same HRW report. status: verified.

## Parents, on the record, under their own names

- **Linda Pee**, mother of **Audrey** (girl), East Webster High School, Webster County School District, Mississippi. Audrey paddled first in sixth grade (one lick from the gym coach, lined up with 10 tardy students, in front of the class; purple bruises, ER visit) and again in March 2007 in twelfth grade (two blows from the principal in his office, for sweatpants that showed her ankles) after her mother had opted her out in writing. Verbatim: "The paddles they use look sort of like a flattened baseball bat – they're a piece of wood about 15 inches long, three inches wide, and an inch thick, with a handle at one end." And: "She said the principal would leave the door open so people could see that he was hitting people." And: "She was paddled in first period and she had bruises all over her by third period." Source: written testimony to the US House Education and Labor subcommittee, 2010-04-15 (PDF, read with pdftotext). status: verified. Note: these are the mother's words; the PDF attributes no direct quote to Audrey.

- **Tessa Davis**, mother, Arkansas (school outside Little Rock, unnamed); chose paddling for her son "C" in kindergarten about eight years ago (he is 13 now). Verbatim: "I was kind of at, just like my breaking point," Davis recalled. "And he was like, 'Well, I was paddled, so give him the paddling.'" "So that's what we chose." "And I just remember feeling like instant regret." Source: NPR, Kadin Mills, 2026-08-20, read at the KCUR republication. status: verified.

- **Venus Maynor**, father of **Orrie**, paddled in second grade at Prospect Elementary School, Public Schools of Robeson County, North Carolina, for running out of line in the hall. Verbatim: "She said 'Hey we're going to paddle you for not doing what we asked you to do'," he said. "And of course she made good on her promise. She paddled him when they got back to the room with a witness." Source: WUNC, Jess Clark, 2017-02-23. status: verified.

## Educators who administered it, or refused to

- **Chris Belcher**, now assistant teaching professor, MU College of Education and Human Development, former Columbia Public Schools superintendent, Missouri. In the 1980s, early in his administrative career, he was told to paddle a special-education student because the administrator who normally handled that student was absent. Verbatim: "The kid screamed, and I felt awful," he said. (The article's "worst experience of his life" is paraphrase.) Source: Columbia Missourian, Sofi Zeman, 2023-04-03, read at The 74 republication (Missouri Independent page returned 403). status: verified.

- **Ralph McClaney**, assistant principal, Carver Middle School, Meridian Public School District, Mississippi; resigned 2003-09-30 rather than paddle a sixth-grade girl on the principal's order. Verbatim: "The idea of a big white guy hitting an 80-pound black girl because she talked back to the teacher did not sit well with me," and "I decided I did not get my master's degree in education to spend my time paddling students." Source: The Washington Post, Michael Dobbs, 2004-02-22, read at The Seattle Times archive. status: verified.

- **Alan Gordon**, elementary principal, Warner Public Schools, Oklahoma, who still administers paddlings (two paddles named "Peace" and "Harmony"). Verbatim: "The shop teacher made my paddles for me," said Gordon. "I sit right here before I even spank them." "They place their hands flat on the desk, they look at the picture and they spread their legs apart." Source: NPR, Kadin Mills, 2026-08-20, read at the KCUR republication. status: verified. Useful as the administrator's-eye view; he defends the practice.
