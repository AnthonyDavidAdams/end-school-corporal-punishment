The Restatement (Second) of Torts is not freely available online; the American Law Institute sells it. Every quotation here was read in a court opinion that quotes it, and the fact file records the words as the courts give them.

Ingraham, 430 U.S. at 661: "The prevalent rule in this country today privileges such force as a teacher or administrator 'reasonably believes to be necessary for (the child's) proper control, training, or education.' Restatement (Second) of Torts § 147(2) (1965); see id., § 153(2)." At 662: "All of the circumstances are to be taken into account in determining whether the punishment is reasonable in a particular case. Among the most important considerations are the seriousness of the offense, the attitude and past behavior of the child, the nature and severity of the punishment, the age and strength of the child, and the availability of less severe but equally effective means of discipline. Id., at 290-291; Restatement (Second) of Torts § 150, Comments c-e, p. 268 (1965)." The "Id." is 1 F. Harper and F. James, Law of Torts § 3.20 (1956), so the phrase "less severe but equally effective means of discipline" is the Court's summary of Harper and James and of the comments, not the Restatement's own sentence.

Rodney C., 91 Misc. 2d at 680-681, gives the Restatement's own words. Section 150 subdivision (b): "the age, sex, physical and mental condition of the child" are factors. Comment d: a parent is not privileged "to use a means to compel obedience if a less severe method appears likely to be equally effective." Comment e: "a punishment may not be unnecessarily degrading." Section 151: force "applied * * * primarily for any purpose other than proper training or education of the child or for the preservation of discipline is not privileged." Rodney C. is a parental case; Ingraham applies the same sections to teachers through section 147(2) and section 153(2).

How to use: quote Ingraham for the teacher's privilege and for the factor list, and quote Rodney C. when the exact Restatement wording of comment d is wanted. Sacks (2009) at 1174 n.34 reproduces Ingraham's factor list, so her "less severe but equally effective means" is Ingraham's phrase, not the Restatement's. Comment c was not quoted in either opinion and has not been read in this project.
