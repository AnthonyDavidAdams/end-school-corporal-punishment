Majority reasons by McLachlin C.J. The passages: paragraph 38, "Contemporary social consensus is that, while teachers may sometimes use corrective force to remove children from classrooms or secure compliance with instructions, the use of corporal punishment by teachers is not acceptable. Many school boards forbid the use of corporal punishment, and some provinces and territories have legislatively prohibited its use by teachers ... Section 43 will protect a teacher who uses reasonable, corrective force to restrain or remove a child in appropriate circumstances. Substantial societal consensus, supported by expert evidence and Canada's treaty obligations, indicates that corporal punishment by teachers is unreasonable." Paragraph 40, "Teachers may reasonably apply force to remove a child from a classroom or secure compliance with instructions, but not merely as corporal punishment." Paragraph 37 records the experts' agreement that corporal punishment of children under two and of teenagers, with objects, or to the head is harmful and "will not be reasonable." The statute was upheld for parents (minor corrective force of a transitory and trifling nature); the court narrowed it by interpretation rather than striking it. Dissents by Binnie, Arbour and Deschamps JJ. Use as the clearest example of a court reading a reasonableness standard against the evidence and removing teachers from it.
