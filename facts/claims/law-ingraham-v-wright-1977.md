Powell for the Court; White dissenting with Brennan, Marshall and Stevens. The Court did acknowledge a 'constitutionally protected liberty interest' in freedom from bodily punishment (at 674) and left the substantive due process question open (at 659 n.12), which is where fifty years of circuit litigation have run.

At 661-662 the Court states the common-law rule it left in place: teachers 'may impose reasonable but not excessive force to discipline a child,' privileging force a teacher 'reasonably believes to be necessary for (the child's) proper control, training, or education' (Restatement (Second) of Torts section 147(2)), with reasonableness judged by 'all of the circumstances,' including 'the availability of less severe but equally effective means of discipline' (section 150, comments c-e). See law-restatement-second-torts-147-150.
