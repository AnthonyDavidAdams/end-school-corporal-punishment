Penal Code section 9.62 in full: "The use of force, but not deadly force, against a person is justified: (1) if the actor is entrusted with the care, supervision, or administration of the person for a special purpose; and (2) when and to the degree the actor reasonably believes the force is necessary to further the special purpose or to maintain discipline in a group." Education Code section 22.0512(a): "A professional employee of a school district may not be subject to disciplinary proceedings for the employee's use of physical force against a student to the extent justified under Section 9.62, Penal Code." Section 22.0512(c): "This section does not prohibit a school district from: (1) enforcing a policy relating to corporal punishment; or (2) notwithstanding Subsection (a), bringing a disciplinary proceeding against a professional employee of the district who violates the district policy relating to corporal punishment." Section 37.0011(a) defines corporal punishment as "the deliberate infliction of physical pain by hitting, paddling, spanking, slapping, or any other physical force used as a means of discipline," excluding athletic activity and authorized restraint; (b) to (d) set the opt-out.

Standard words: "reasonably believes the force is necessary" (Penal Code), "to the extent justified under Section 9.62" (Education Code). Texas is the one state in this set whose statute makes reasonable belief in necessity the explicit condition of the justification, in the Restatement's own terms. Texas Education Code section 22.0511 (immunity from civil liability for acts within the scope of duties involving judgment or discretion, with an exception for excessive force in discipline) was not re-read on this date and is not quoted here.
