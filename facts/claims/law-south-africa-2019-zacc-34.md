Mogoeng CJ for a unanimous court of ten. The case concerned parents, not schools, and the order (paragraph 76) declares "the common law defence of reasonable and moderate parental chastisement" inconsistent with sections 10 (dignity) and 12(1)(c) (freedom from all forms of violence) of the Constitution. The reasoning that transfers to a necessity argument: paragraph 39, "we must ask why it is necessary to resort to chastisement in the first place. Is it not the actual or potential pain or hurt that flows from it that is believed to be more likely to have a greater effect than any other reasonably available method of discipline?"; paragraph 64, "the paucity of clear or satisfactory empirical evidence that supports chastisement as a beneficial means of instilling discipline"; paragraph 67, force "cannot in circumstances where there is an effective non-violent option available be said to be consonant with the best interests of a child"; paragraph 69, positive parenting "is a less restrictive means of discipline that could potentially be effective in the attainment of the same purpose"; paragraph 70 as quoted in the claim. The court was candid (paragraphs 53, 54 and 64) that some of the research does not separate moderate from abusive force, which is the honest caveat to carry when citing it. The judgment cites the American Academy of Pediatrics 2018 statement (footnote 50).
