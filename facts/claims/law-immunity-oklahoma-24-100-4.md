70 O.S. section 24-100.4(C): "The teacher of a child attending a public school shall have the same right as a parent or guardian to control and discipline such child according to district policies during the time the child is in attendance or in transit to or from the school or any other school function authorized by the school district or classroom presided over by the teacher." Subsection (D): "Except concerning students on individualized education plans (IEP) pursuant to the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), P.L. No. 101-476, the State Board of Education shall not have authority to prescribe student disciplinary policies for school districts or to proscribe corporal punishment in the public schools." 21 O.S. section 844 (Laws 1963, c. 53, section 2): "Provided, however, that nothing contained in this Act shall prohibit any parent, teacher or other person from using ordinary force as a means of discipline, including but not limited to spanking, switching or paddling." 70 O.S. section 13-116(A) (Laws 2017, c. 222; amended Laws 2025, c. 99): "School district personnel shall be prohibited from using corporal punishment on any student identified with a disability in accordance with the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA)"; (B) defines corporal punishment as "the deliberate infliction of physical pain by hitting, paddling, spanking, slapping, or any other physical force used as a means of discipline."

Standard words: "ordinary force," "according to district policies." The parent's right that section 24-100.4(C) borrows is itself the common-law privilege, so the Restatement's reasonableness and necessity limits apply through the borrowing. Section 6-114, cited in older sources, was renumbered as 24-100.4 by Laws 2002, c. 149.
