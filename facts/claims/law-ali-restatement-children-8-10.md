Black letter as posted by the ALI in April 2019 (Part II, Children in Schools; Chapter 8, Discipline and Order Maintenance; Topic 1; section 8.10, Use of Force to Control and Punish), first paragraph: "In a criminal proceeding or a civil suit alleging an intentional tort, the use of reasonable force by a teacher or other school authority with responsibility for the care and education of students is privileged if the school authority reasonably believes that the force is necessary to maintain order and safety in the school." Second paragraph as in the claim.

Comment c (page 145): "Where a broad and growing legal, medical, and social scientific consensus categorically rejects the propriety and efficacy of corporal punishment, corporal punishment cannot be said to be 'necessary.' Thus, application of the 'reasonable belief in necessity' requirement precludes application of the common-law privilege to corporal punishment." Comment c also says that "in both the school and family setting, there is a growing social scientific consensus that the use of corporal punishment is ineffective and potentially harmful to children." Comment a (page 143): "Where corporal punishment is still authorized by statute, this Restatement notes that the reasonableness contemplated in those statutes is increasingly out of step with trends in the law."

Status of the text. The PDF read here carries the footer "Tentative draft – not approved" because it was posted before the May 2019 Annual Meeting. The ALI's press release of May 17, 2024 says the membership "voted today to approve Restatement of the Law, Children and the Law" and that "until the official text is published, the drafts approved by the membership are the official position of ALI, and may be cited as such." The ALI publications page lists the Restatement as a 2024 publication in volumes. The published volume was not opened in this project (it is sold, not posted), so the section number and wording in the official text have not been checked against the 2019 draft; every citation should say "Tentative Draft No. 2 (2019), Restatement approved by the membership May 17, 2024" until someone reads the printed section.
