Verbatim: "The Diocese of Des Moines Catholic Schools Office, under the aegis of the Bishop, and the State of Iowa define corporal punishment as the intentional physical punishment of a student. Corporal punishment is prohibited. It includes the use of unreasonable or unnecessary physical force or physical contact made with the intent to harm or cause pain." Followed by the list of permitted reasonable force (quell a disturbance, obtain a weapon, self-defense under Iowa Code 704.3, protection of property under 704.4 or 704.5, remove a disruptive pupil, protect from self-harm, protect others, incidental contact to maintain order) and five factors for judging reasonableness. No adoption date is printed on the policy.

Access note: on 2026-10-29 the live URL at dmdiocese.org returned a 404 page in both WebFetch and the browser, and the diocese's policies index page no longer lists the 400-series documents in a form the browser could find; the text above was read from the Internet Archive's 2024-03-03 capture of the diocese's own PDF. Iowa is not a state that permits corporal punishment in public schools (Iowa Code 280.21), so this policy restates state law for diocesan schools; cite it as an example of a diocesan rule, not as a rule inside a permitting state. The lead that all US diocesan Catholic schools prohibit it (Center for Effective Discipline, 2011) remains a secondary, unverified claim.
