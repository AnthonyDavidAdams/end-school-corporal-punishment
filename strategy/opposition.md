# Every argument for corporal punishment we have heard, and what has answered it

Add to this file after every hearing and board meeting, with the source. Responses cite `facts/claims/` ids where a number is involved.

| Argument | Where it is made | Response that has worked |
|---|---|---|
| "Local control: let districts decide." | State hearings | Agree. Most districts already decided; name the count from `data/districts/`. The bill ratifies their choice and protects the students in the holdouts. At the board level, this argument is on our side: the board is the local control. |
| "Parental rights: parents want the option." | Boards, hearings | Agree that parents decide. That is why written opt-in consent is the minimum; a school employee should never hit a child whose parent did not ask. Then: parents who consent can do so at home; the question is whether the state's employee does it. |
| "It works; I was paddled and turned out fine." | Everywhere | The person saying it turned out fine and also stopped being paddled. The students who get paddled are paddled repeatedly (district's own data, by student, from the records request). If it worked, it would not need repeating. Meta-analysis: `study-gershoff-grogan-kaylor-2016`. |
| "Then what do we do? Suspensions will go up." | Boards | Neighbor district that stopped, with its numbers. `training/`. Suspension data in `facts/` for states after bans. |
| "It's our tradition / faith." | Boards | Do not argue faith. Bring the pastor from the district. Note which denominations' bodies have spoken against it, from `facts/claims/org-*.md`. |
| "Teachers need it to keep order." | Boards | NEA and AFT oppose it (`org-nea`, `org-aft`). Teachers do not administer it in most policies; principals do. Bring a teacher. |
| "It's rare; only a few kids." | Hearings | The district's own count by school. Rarity is an argument for ending it: nothing is lost. |
| "This is outsiders telling us what to do." | Boards | Only constituents speak. No outside speakers, ever. See the code of conduct. |
| "The alternatives cost money." | Boards, hearings | The training is free; the practices are the state's own MTSS framework; Title IV-A already funds it. Liability costs money: the case in `case-studies/` from this state or circuit. |
| "Parents can opt out already." | Hearings | Opt-out puts the burden on the parent who was never told. Records requests show how many refusals are on file versus how many parents knew they could. |
