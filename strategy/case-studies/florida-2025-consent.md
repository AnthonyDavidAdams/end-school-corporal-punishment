# Florida, HB 1255 (2025): opt-in consent

**Result:** since July 1, 2025 a district that authorizes corporal punishment must obtain written parental consent (for the year or per instance), have a second informed adult present, provide a written explanation on request, and review the policy at a public meeting every three years or the authorization expires. Ch. 2025-110; Fla. Stat. § 1002.20(4)(c).

**Votes:** House 86-27 (April 23); Senate 38-0 (May 1); House concurrence 100-0 and Senate 30-0 (May 2); signed May 30, 2025. Vehicle: an omnibus education bill by Reps. Dana Trabulsy and Chase Tramont (R).

**Who pushed it:** University of Florida students in the Florida Student Policy Forum (Graham Bernstein, Jacob Kaplan), over four years, after a Levy County student was struck without parental consent. They first sought a statewide ban for students with disabilities; "more conservative communities pushed back," so they settled for consent. Bernstein: "You can't just imprint your perspective onto other people."

**Baseline:** 17 of 67 districts reported 516 instances in 2023-24 (Suwannee 74, Holmes 65, Calhoun 59, Columbia 50, Wakulla 45, Washington 43); 647 students in the 2021-22 federal count. Post-law data not yet available.

**Critique:** the law codifies paddling with a form; an attorney quoted by Newsweek noted the inequality it creates between consenting and non-consenting families.

**Lesson:** consent is the parental-rights version of a ban and passes unanimously where a ban cannot. It concentrates use in a shrinking set of rural districts, which become the next district-level targets, and it puts a form in every parent's hands. Ride an omnibus when the standalone bill will not get a hearing.
