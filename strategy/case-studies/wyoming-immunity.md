# Wyoming: one principal, 48 policies, and an immunity clause

**The law:** W.S. § 21-4-308 lets each district board adopt "reasonable forms of punishment" and, in subsection (b), grants teachers, principals and superintendents civil and criminal immunity "in the exercise of reasonable corporal discipline of a student as authorized by board policy." The statute does not itself authorize the practice; the immunity is the whole of it.

**The campaign:** Jeff Jones, principal of Tongue River Middle School, reviewed all 48 district policies for his 2021 dissertation and found nine still allowed corporal punishment, most without knowing it. After his outreach, by September 8, 2022 only Niobrara County School District #1 still had it in policy (later reports said three; the counts conflict). Wyoming had reported no incidents to the federal government since 2012.

**The bills:** SF0047 (2023, Joint Education Interim Committee) would have repealed only the immunity. Senate Education 5-0, Senate 26-5, House Education 9-0, then the House Committee of the Whole failed it 12-47 on February 14, 2023 after Rep. Barry Crago argued it could stop teachers from breaking up fights. HB0065 (2026) bundled the repeal with mandatory discipline policies, parental notice and reporting; it failed introduction 38-24 on February 9, 2026, short of the two-thirds a budget session requires. Earlier attempts failed in 2001, 2002 and 2003.

**Lesson:** one credible insider with a complete policy inventory moved nearly every district without a vote of the legislature. The immunity clause is a liability shield that boards value; a repeal bill needs an explicit reasonable-force exception for breaking up fights, or the argument that killed SF0047 will kill the next one.
