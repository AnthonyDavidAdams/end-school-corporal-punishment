# Kentucky: the regulatory route to zero

**Result:** all 171 districts had prohibiting policies by November 2, 2023; zero incidents in 2023-24. The statute (KRS 503.110) still permits the practice, so the map shows Kentucky as "partial."

**Trend:** about 1,100 incidents in 2011-12; 452 in 2017-18; 284 in 2018-19; 142 in 2019-20 (about 46 percent involving students with disabilities); 5 in 2022-23 (all Pike County); 0 in 2023-24. By 2018-19, 156 districts already prohibited it.

**The bills that failed:** Rep. Steve Riley (R-Glasgow), a retired educator, filed ban bills from 2018. HB 22 (2020) passed the House 65-17 and died in Senate Education. HB 119 (2022) died in committee. No 2023 bill was located.

**The regulation that worked:** the Kentucky Board of Education adopted 704 KAR 7:170 (approved 11-0 in December 2021, effective August 30, 2022). Every district had to adopt a policy that either prohibited corporal punishment or permitted it only with: annual written consent plus verbal consent before each instance; no use on students with IEPs or 504 plans, homeless or foster youth; a same-gender certified witness; no instruments; 30 minutes of counseling by the next school day; full documentation. Commissioner Jason Glass: "I consider this a barbaric practice." Faced with the compliance burden, every district chose prohibition; Pike County was last.

**Coalition:** Prevent Child Abuse Kentucky (Jill Seyfred), Kentucky Youth Advocates (Terry Brooks), student advocate Alex Young (began lobbying in seventh grade; now on the End Hitting USA board), Rep. Riley, the Department and Board of Education, pediatrician Kelly Dauk, ACLU of Kentucky (racial-disparity framing).

**Caveat from the coalition:** Terry Brooks: "to think that no paddle is ever swung in Kentucky is aspirational, but inaccurate." Advocates still want a statute because policies can be reversed.

**Lesson:** when the Senate will not move, a state board of education can make the practice so expensive to administer that districts quit on their own. Every state where the practice is legal has a state board with rulemaking authority over discipline; this lever has been tried in one state and worked completely. It belongs in the state playbook next to the bill.
