# North Carolina: district by district to zero

**Result:** all 115 districts prohibited corporal punishment by October 2, 2018. G.S. 115C-390.4 still permits it, so the map shows "partial." 2025 ban bills (HB 983, S714) sit in Rules committees.

**The statute that emptied the practice (2011, S.L. 2011-282, amended 2022):** local boards decide, but must have a witness, give written parental notice, keep records, report annually to the State Board by race, gender and disability, and offer a written parental opt-out at enrollment. The opt-out carries a sting: an opted-out student "may be subject to suspension" for offenses that otherwise would not require it. A 2013 amendment barring use on students with disabilities when a parent objects is often cited but was not verified here.

**The last two:** Robeson County voted to end it in April 2018 after more than two decades of advocacy led by NC Child (Tom Vitaglione) and a change in board leadership. Graham County (Robbinsville High School, 34 incidents in 2016-17) voted unanimously on October 2, 2018.

**State-level attempts:** a 2019 bill by Reps. Susan Fisher (D) and Linda Johnson (R) cleared House Education with bipartisan support ("educating should be left up to the schools, and discipline should be left up to the parents") and went no further. HB 983 (Reps. Cervania and Morey) and S714 (Sen. Robinson, with Republican Sen. Kevin Corbin among sponsors), backed by the State Board of Education and NC PTA, were parked in Rules in spring 2025.

**Political weather:** the 2024 Republican gubernatorial nominee, Mark Robinson, described "beating them in a circle" as love and declined to say whether it belongs in schools; the winner, Josh Stein, opposed it.

**Lesson:** an opt-out and reporting statute makes every district's numbers public every year and gives every parent a form; twenty years of one organization's pressure did the rest. A legislature can decline to codify a ban even at zero use, so the district route has to be finished, not started, before counting on the statute.
