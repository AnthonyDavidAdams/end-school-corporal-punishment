# New Mexico, HB 172 (2011)

**Result:** NMSA 1978 § 22-5-4.3(B): "Corporal punishment shall be prohibited by each local school board and each governing body of a charter school." Signed April 6, 2011 by Gov. Susana Martinez (R), Laws 2011 ch. 97. (Older sources cite "SB 199"; that bill does not exist for this purpose. SB 32 of 2011 was a voting bill.)

**Votes:** House 36-31 (March 8); Senate 22-17 (March 18). Sponsor Rep. Rick Miera (D).

**Preconditions:** only 36 of 89 districts still permitted it; the state's last data year (2006) showed 705 incidents.

**Argument that moved the governor:** parental rights. Martinez: "The decision on whether or not to use corporal punishment on a child is one that is best left to a parent." Supporters also argued the practice sent the wrong message about resolving conflict and fell on students with disabilities, poor and minority students.

**Opposition:** the threat of physical punishment is needed for discipline; local boards, not the state, should decide.

**Lesson:** a narrowly passed bill from the other party can be signed by a conservative governor if the signing statement is about parents, not the state. Write the parental-rights version of the argument before the bill reaches the governor's desk.
