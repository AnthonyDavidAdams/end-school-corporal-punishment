# Idaho, House Bill 281 (2023)

**Result:** Idaho Code § 33-1224 amended: "corporal punishment shall not be used"; chemical restraint banned; physical restraint and seclusion limited to imminent danger; annual staff training in positive behavior supports and de-escalation required. Signed April 3, 2023, effective July 1, 2023. Public and charter schools only; private schools were removed by Senate amendment.

**Votes:** House 45-22 (March 9); Senate 21-12 as amended (March 28); House concurrence 44-25 (March 30).

**Sponsor:** House Education Committee; floor sponsor Rep. Marco Erickson (R-Idaho Falls). Supported by Gov. Brad Little and Superintendent Debbie Critchfield. An earlier print, HB 140, was referred to committee and replaced.

**What created the record:** an Idaho Statesman investigation (Becca Savransky) documenting injuries and trauma from restraint and seclusion. The state Department of Education then surveyed districts and found more than a third had no policy and about a quarter had no reporting system.

**Witnesses:** a former second-grade teacher ("I regret every single minute to this day") and a foster mother whose son was secluded over her objection.

**Opposition:** Sen. Scott Herndon (R) wanted private schools out (they were removed); Sen. Tammy Nichols (R) wanted a parental-notice clause; the sponsor said some opponents misunderstood chemical restraint.

**Lesson:** corporal punishment was barely contested because it rode inside a bill about a problem the legislature had just been shown in the newspaper. A Republican sponsor, a governor's support, and a state survey did the rest. Bundling works where a standalone ban would draw a culture-war fight.
