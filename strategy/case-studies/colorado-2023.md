# Colorado, HB23-1191 (2023)

**Result:** corporal punishment prohibited in public schools (including charters) and licensed child care, C.R.S. § 22-1-140. Signed and effective April 20, 2023. Private schools not covered.

**Votes:** House Education 8-2 (March 9); House 48-16-1 (March 15); Senate Education 6-0 (March 22); Senate 32-0 (March 28).

**Sponsors:** Rep. Regina English (D-Colorado Springs), Sen. Rhonda Fields (D-Aurora); 27 House and 13 Senate co-sponsors.

**Preconditions:** 154 of 178 districts already prohibited it by policy. The state collected no data, and the federal Office for Civil Rights recorded zero Colorado complaints in 2017-18, so opponents could not argue that anything would change and sponsors could argue that nothing would be lost.

**Coalition:** Disability Law Colorado and Mental Health Colorado testified; pediatrician Lora Melnicoe was credited by the AAP policy-statement lead author as instrumental; Lives in the Balance ran an action alert.

**Argument that moved votes:** closing a loophole and sending a message to administrators, paired with disability advocates reporting bruises on students and the research on depression and suicidality. Three House Republicans voted yes (Pugliese, Bradley, Hartsook).

**Opposition:** Rep. Ken DeGraaf (R): "Pain is a primal motivator. You learn via pain," and districts should decide locally. Rep. Mary Bradfield (R): legislators lack classroom experience and well-meaning fixes backfire. A similar 2017 bill had been stalled by Republicans.

**Lesson:** in a near-zero-use state, a ban is cheap to pass once the majority exists; the disability framing brought a few votes from the minority. Do not wait for data that does not exist; the absence of data is itself the argument that the ban costs nothing.
