# Missouri legislators: two held notes

Status: HELD. Not sent. Nothing in this file goes out from the repository; if Anthony sends it, it goes from his own mail in his own voice, one note per person, logged in `crm/dossiers/MO/legislature.yaml` under `contact_log`. The people and facts below are from that dossier, read 2026-10-29; the 104th General Assembly convenes in January 2027 and the chairs may change, so check the roster before sending.

Facts the notes rest on (all from the repository on 2026-10-29): three hundred twenty-five Missouri boards on the record; one hundred twenty-five prohibit corporal punishment in writing; one hundred twenty-eight permit it (forty-six allow, eighty-two by written parental consent); seventy-two of the permitting boards reported zero students paddled in 2023-24; one thousand and two Missouri students were paddled in 2023-24 (federal Civil Rights Data Collection). Four House bills would have required every district policy to prohibit it: HB 121 and HB 160 (2023, referred to Elementary and Secondary Education on May 12, 2023, the last day of session), HB 1581 (2024, referred to General Laws on May 17, 2024), HB 2827 (2026, referred to Emerging Issues on May 15, 2026). None was heard. The 2022 written-permission sentence now in RSMo 160.261 entered SB 681 through the House committee substitute, which the Elementary and Secondary Education Committee voted out nineteen to nothing on April 21, 2022.

## Note 1: the committee chairs

Two recipients, one note each, same shape, the frame tuned to each chair's own words in the dossier.

### 1a. Rep. Ed Lewis, Chair, House Committee on Elementary and Secondary Education

To: Ed.Lewis@house.mo.gov (official, published). Frame: teachers (his own words are about teachers and public education) with the fiscal frame as a second door. Do not lead with faith; he has said nothing on it in the sources read.

Subject: A question about corporal punishment bills, and an offer of the district record

Chairman Lewis,

I keep a public record of what every Missouri school district's own written policy says about corporal punishment, quoted from the districts' documents, at earthpilot.org/kids. I am writing with one question and one offer, and no ask beyond them.

The question. Four bills in the last four sessions would have asked every district to put in its policy what one hundred twenty-five Missouri boards have already put in theirs, on their own, under the local control RSMo 160.261 gives them. None of the four had a hearing; each was referred in the last week of session. I do not assume that was anyone's decision in particular. But it means the committee has never been shown evidence either way on the question the bills raise, which is whether the practice is necessary in the one hundred twenty-eight districts that keep it when it plainly is not in the one hundred twenty-five that do not. Has the committee ever seen such evidence, from the districts or from the Department? If it has, I would like to read it and put it in the record next to the policies. If it has not, I would like to know whether a hearing on the question, apart from any bill, is something you would consider.

The offer. Everything in the record is free to the committee: the policy of every district with its source, the federal count by district, the seventy-two districts whose policy permits it but whose schools reported none, and what the teachers and principals in districts that stopped say about what replaced it. You spent thirty-two years in a Missouri classroom, and the people I hear from most are teachers who do not want to be the one holding the paddle. If the committee or its staff want any of it in a form that fits a hearing, say so and it will be ready.

Thank you for your time and for the work on the committee.

### 1b. Sen. Rick Brattin, Chair, Senate Committee on Education

To: no Senate email is published; Capitol phone 573-751-2108, or the contact form on his Senate page (senate.mo.gov/Senators/Member?id=5). Frame: parental rights and freedom from government, the only frame his record supports. Do not use the human-rights or international frame anywhere in the note.

Subject: A question about corporal punishment in Missouri schools, and an offer of the district record

Chairman Brattin,

I keep a public record of what every Missouri school district's own written policy says about corporal punishment, quoted from the districts' documents, at earthpilot.org/kids. One question and one offer.

The question. In one hundred twenty-eight Missouri districts a parent can be handed a form asking permission for a school employee to paddle their child; in the other one hundred twenty-five the question never comes up, because the board wrote the practice out of its policy on its own. The House has had four bills in four sessions to make that the rule statewide, and none was heard. The Senate has never been asked. So no committee of the General Assembly has yet seen evidence on whether the practice is necessary anywhere, when more than half the state's boards have decided it is not. Has the Education Committee ever been shown such evidence? If so I would like to read it. If not, would the committee hear the question, from the districts and the parents, apart from any bill?

The offer. The record is free to the committee: every district's policy with its source, the federal count of students paddled by district (one thousand and two Missouri students in 2023-24), the seventy-two districts whose policy permits it but whose schools reported none, and what the districts that stopped did instead. Senator Nurrenbern, who sits on your committee, carried two of the House bills; I am writing to her separately. Nothing here needs to be a mandate on anyone; the parents I hear from want to know the state will not hit their child without their say, and most Missouri boards already agree.

Thank you for your time.

## Note 2: the sponsors (ally note)

Recipients, from the dossier: Sen. Maggie Nurrenbern (D-17; sponsor of HB 121 in 2023 and HB 1581 in 2024; now on Senate Education; Capitol phone 573-751-5282, no published email), Rep. Jo Doll (D-91; sponsor of HB 2827 in 2026 and the sole co-sponsor of HB 121; Jo.Doll@house.mo.gov), and Ian Mackey (sponsor of HB 1937 in 2022 and HB 160 in 2023; resigned from the House effective August 31, 2026, so he cannot file in 2027; send only if Anthony wants him as an outside witness, with the second paragraph adjusted). One note each, the bill numbers filled in.

Subject: Hearing material for a corporal punishment bill, ready when you want it

[Senator Nurrenbern / Representative Doll],

You carried [HB 121 and HB 1581 / HB 2827], which would have asked every Missouri district to prohibit corporal punishment in its written policy. I keep a public record of what each district's policy already says, quoted from its own documents (earthpilot.org/kids), and I want to offer it to you for the next time the bill is filed, as hearing material and nothing else.

What is in it, as of this fall: three hundred twenty-five Missouri boards on the record. One hundred twenty-five already prohibit the practice in writing, so the bill asks nothing of them. One hundred twenty-eight permit it, and seventy-two of those reported to the federal government that no student was paddled in 2023-24; for them the bill writes down what their schools already do. The federal count for the rest was one thousand and two Missouri students in 2023-24, concentrated in a few counties, and the record has it by district. There is also a two-page board brief and model policy that Missouri boards are being offered directly, the research in one place with every figure sourced, and accounts of how Colorado and Idaho passed their bans in 2023 and who testified.

Two things from the record that may be useful at a hearing. First, the opt-in sentence in RSMo 160.261 came out of the Elementary and Secondary Education Committee nineteen to nothing in 2022; the committee has already agreed once that the paddle is the parent's call, and the bill is the next step in that logic. Second, the question no committee has been asked to answer is what evidence any district has that the practice is necessary; I am asking a small number of boards that question directly this fall, respectfully, and will share what they say.

If any of this would help, tell me the form that fits (a one-page fact sheet, witness contacts among Missouri teachers and pediatricians, the district list for your colleagues' districts) and it will be ready before filing. Nothing in it costs anything and all of it is public.

Thank you for carrying it.
