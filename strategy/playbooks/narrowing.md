# Playbook: narrowing where a ban cannot pass yet

Each step below cuts use sharply on its own and builds the record for the next. They are listed in the order they have passed in practice.

| Step | What it does | Where it has passed | Why it passes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disability exemption | No corporal punishment for students with an IEP or 504 plan | Louisiana (2017), Tennessee (2018, with a parental-permission exception), Mississippi (2019), Oklahoma (2025, after failing 45-43 in 2023) | Federal disparity data; nobody defends paddling a child with autism on the record. Mississippi's count of students with disabilities struck fell from 3,304 to 30 |
| Opt-in consent | Written parental permission required in advance, renewed each year | Florida (HB 1255, 2025, unanimous final votes); Kentucky by regulation (2022) | Parental-rights frame; it is the parental-rights bill |
| Immunity repeal | Remove the statutory liability shield for staff who paddle | Attempted in Wyoming 2023 (failed 12-47) and 2026 (failed introduction) | Liability; needs an explicit reasonable-force exception or it dies on the fight-breaking argument |
| Administration limits | Principal only, another adult present, written record, parent notified same day | Many district policies; some state rules | Liability and order; boards like paperwork that protects them |
| Public reporting | Annual count by school on the district website | Local policy in several states | Transparency is hard to vote against |
| Age limits | No use above or below certain grades | Some district policies | Splits the opposition |

Each step is a district policy amendment or a one-section bill. Ask for the one the dossier says has the votes. Log the result in `case-studies/`.
