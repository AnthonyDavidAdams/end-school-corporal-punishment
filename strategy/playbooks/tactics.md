# Tactics: every move we could make, by who it is aimed at

A brainstorm, written 2026-10-26, for the game. The score is children covered by a written ban (`data/game/score.json`). Every tactic below is a move the engine can make, measure, and keep or retire. Each line gives the move, why it might work, how we would know, and what it needs. Tags: **have** (built and in the repo), **build** (we can make it), **person** (needs a human we do not have yet). The rules that bind all of it are at the end; `persuasion.md` has the evidence behind the framing.

The one idea under all of it: a district ends corporal punishment when the superintendent decides it is easier to end it than to keep it, and the board decides nobody will punish them for the vote. Every tactic either lowers the cost of ending it or raises the cost of keeping it, for one of those people.

## 1. Superintendents

The person who writes the agenda. One relationship here is worth a hundred emails to anyone else.

1. **The friend's note** (have). A plain ask for the policy. Already the best-performing move: 17 percent reply, 16 percent produce a document within 14 days. Variants to test: who we are up front; link-only ask; a Sunday-evening send.
2. **The invitation** (have, under test). "Your policy permits it, your reports say zero uses; here is the one sentence 291 Texas boards adopted." Zero replies from 38 sends so far: the attachment and the length are the first suspects. Variant B is the sentence inline with a link.
3. **The certificate** (have). Recognition for districts that already ban. A district that is praised publicly stays banned, and its superintendent becomes a reference. Test: ask certified superintendents for one sentence we can quote to their neighbors.
4. **The peer reference** (build). "Your neighbor in Hall County ended it last spring; here is what their superintendent said." Social proof from the next county over, by name, with permission. The 204 flipped districts are the reference pool; the neighbor list is `data/game/next-targets.json`.
5. **The inservice gift** (have). The Texas and Arkansas decks and the one-hour training, offered free for the next staff day. Gets us into the building before the policy conversation. Measure: downloads and the follow-up reply.
6. **The liability memo** (have). "Should liability pools care": the pool's own exposure, the case law, the premium question. Test on superintendents whose district self-insures or sits in a pool that has been asked.
7. **The parent-request handout** (have). The one page for the day a parent asks for the paddle. Positions ending it as the data-driven choice and gives the principal words. A reason to reply even when they are not ready to change policy.
8. **The ninety-day plan** (have, in the kit). The whole transition pre-written so the superintendent's cost is reading, not writing.
9. **The 1:1 relationship** (build). For the fifty largest permitting districts, a named sequence over months: the ask, the thank-you, the brief, a relevant article, a note after a board meeting, an offer to join a call. One touch per ten business days, written by the model against the whole thread, every touch logged. The sales cadence, used honestly.
10. **The phone call** (build, person optional). The phone agent can place a call to the superintendent's office to ask where the policy lives and whether the board has discussed it. A human voice from Anthony is better for the top twenty.
11. **The question they cannot answer** (have, in the writer). "When is there a reason to believe it will be helpful?" and "what does your board rely on?" Asked once, politely, and recorded. The absence of an answer is itself a public fact.
12. **The records request** (have). The formal ask when the plain one is refused. Raises the cost of silence. Already running with fee rules.
13. **The mastermind call** (person). A free monthly call for administrators hosted by a former dean of education and a former head of special education. Superintendents come for the peers, leave with the plan. Needs the two hosts.
14. **The pilot offer** (build). "Suspend it for one semester, we will help you measure referrals, and you decide in May." Reversible, small, measurable; the foot in the door.
15. **The job-market frame** (build). Superintendents move districts. A ban on the resume is an asset in the districts they want next. Said once, lightly, by a peer.

## 2. Principals and assistant principals

They swing the paddle, and they decide whether a policy on paper becomes a practice.

16. **The handout and the coach** (have). Give them the words for the parent and a coach for the hard student. A principal who has a replacement stops asking for the paddle.
17. **Principal-level asks** (build). In districts where the superintendent is silent, ask the high school principal for the handbook and the practice. Different gatekeeper, same record.
18. **The zero-use letter** (build). To principals in zero-use districts: "your school has not used it in years; the policy still names you as the one who would. Here is how other principals got it off the books." Makes the principal the ally who asks the superintendent.
19. **Principal associations** (person). State principal associations' newsletters and conferences; the training as a session.

## 3. School boards

They vote. They fear the parent who shouts at the meeting more than the evidence.

20. **The board note** (build, next). Two weeks after the superintendent's invitation, a short note to each member we have (826 on file) with the film link, tagged per member. Measure clicks per board.
21. **The film** (have). Four minutes, two girls, same background, one paddled. Built for the board member who has never thought about it.
22. **Public comment by residents** (person, via Ground Crew). Three minutes at the meeting by a parent in the district, story then one number then the ask. The persuasion playbook says this is what actually moves a member. The crew finds the resident; we write nothing for them.
23. **Agenda request** (build). Many boards accept written agenda requests from residents. A one-page request to put policy review on next month's agenda, filed by a resident, is a small reversible ask.
24. **The opt-out count** (have, template). Parents file written refusals; the district must honor them; we count. A board that sees forty refusals knows the practice is already ending.
25. **Board member dossiers** (have, skill). Stated values, profession, church, kids, prior votes. The message is framed in their values: parental rights, local control, order, liability, faith. Public positions only.
26. **The election calendar** (build). Members up for election answer surveys. A one-question candidate survey, published: "Would you vote to end corporal punishment?" Candidates who say yes get the win; incumbents who say no get a public record.
27. **Model policy with their numbering** (have). The solicitor's cost is zero when the policy arrives in the district's own format.
28. **Narrowing votes** (have, playbook). Where a full ban will not pass: disability exemption, opt-in consent, principal-only, mandatory reporting. Each vote makes the next easier.
29. **Praise in public** (build). When a board moves, a thank-you on the site, a certificate, a press note. The reward is public, the pressure was private.

## 4. Teachers and staff

Nobody likes being the one holding the paddle.

30. **Teacher survey** (build). Anonymous, one question, district by district: "Have you ever been asked to administer or witness corporal punishment, and did you want to?" Results shared with the board.
31. **The coach for teachers** (have). The Behavior Coach is the replacement in their pocket. Usage by district is a signal of readiness.
32. **Union locals** (person). Both national unions endorsed the federal ban bill. A state affiliate letter to its locals in permitting districts, with the training attached.
33. **The witness clause** (build). Policies usually require a witness. Staff who decline to witness end the practice without a vote. A quiet note on what the policy requires and what it does not.
34. **Colleges of education** (person). Pre-service teachers trained on the replacement practices; faculty who sign a statement to the districts that hire their graduates.
35. **Staff inservice** (have). The hour of training, free, delivered by Zoom with Co-Pilot running the room.

## 5. Students

They are the ones hit, and the ones who will be parents.

36. **Student press** (build). High school newspapers in permitting districts; a student editor asking the superintendent for the policy is a story the local paper picks up.
37. **Student board representatives** (build). Many boards seat a student member. A note to them with the film.
38. **Youth voice without naming anyone** (rule). We never name or quote a minor. Stories come from adults about their own childhood.
39. **The handbook line** (have, in the record). Students read the handbook. A district whose handbook still lists paddling teaches every student something. The record quotes it; the share kit shows it.
40. **Student clubs and civics classes** (build). A records request is a civics project. A one-page lesson plan: find your district's policy, compare it to the state law, write the board.

## 6. Parents and PTA

The parent who asked for the paddle and the parent who refuses it are both the audience.

41. **The opt-out letter** (have). The written refusal, honored without exception. The first thing any parent in a permitting district can do today.
42. **The parent handout** (have). What the principal says when a parent asks for it, which is also what we say to the parent: data-driven schools, guidance not a vote.
43. **The Behavior Coach** (have). The replacement for the parent who spanks because nothing else worked. A coach that works earns the right to say stop.
44. **PTA and PTO presidents** (build). The national PTA has a position; the local unit rarely knows. A note with the position, the film, and the opt-out letter.
45. **The resident finder** (have, Ground Crew). The crew matches residents in a district to the asks: public comment, agenda request, opt-out, a letter to the editor.
46. **Pediatricians** (person). The AAP's 2018 and 2023 positions, delivered by the pediatrician in the district to their patients' parents and to the board. The AAP chapter is the messenger.
47. **Faith leaders** (person). Where boards cite faith, pastors who oppose hitting children are the only messengers that work. Never argue scripture ourselves.
48. **Targeted ads** (build; pixels in place). Meta and Google to parents in the zip codes of the top targets: the film, the opt-out letter, the coach. Retargeting of board-page visitors. Measure opt-outs filed and coach sessions by region.
49. **Parent stories** (build). Adults in the district telling their own paddling story, on video, with consent, for the board meeting. Never about a child.

## 7. Local and regional media

A flip is a story; a refusal is a story; the numbers by school are a story.

50. **The flip release** (build). When a district moves, a release to the local paper and the state education reporters the same day, with the superintendent's quote. Press list in `data/media/`.
51. **The county map story** (build). "Which districts in our county still paddle": a pre-written local story with the map, offered to the paper.
52. **The data story** (have, numbers). Texas reports by district and by race and sex, from the state's own data. A reporter's story written for them with every figure sourced.
53. **The refusal story** (build). A district that refuses to release its own discipline policy is a records story for the paper.
54. **Letters to the editor** (Ground Crew). Residents, their own words, one number.
55. **Public radio and podcasts** (person). State education reporters at public radio; the mastermind hosts as guests.
56. **Op-eds by the flipped** (person). The superintendent who ended it writes the op-ed; we draft nothing in their name, we ask and we edit.
57. **Social recap** (have). The daily and weekly post with the number and the newest districts recognized.

## 8. State level

The district wins are the proof; the law is the finish.

58. **State education departments** (have, running). Records requests for discipline data and policy lists; fourteen filed.
59. **Legislators with the map** (have, playbook). The map of their own district, the numbers, the neighbors that stopped. Bill watch per state.
60. **State board of education rules** (have, case study). Kentucky ended it by regulation, not statute. The regulatory path in each state.
61. **School board associations** (person). Their model policies set the default for hundreds of districts. One model policy change flips a state's defaults. TASB, ASBA, MSBA and the rest.
62. **Liability pools and insurers** (have, memo). Pools that price the risk, or exclude it, end the practice faster than any letter. Two emails drafted and waiting.
63. **Governors** (have, certificates). State certificates already go to governors; a governor's letter to permitting districts is the ask.
64. **Attorney general opinions** (build). Where the statute is ambiguous, a requested opinion can narrow it.

## 9. Peer and network effects

65. **Neighbor contagion** (have, targets). Work outward from every flip: same county, adjacent counties, same athletic conference. The next-targets list is this.
66. **Athletic conferences and superintendent networks** (build). Superintendents meet at games and regional meetings. A one-line fact for the host of the next regional superintendents' meeting.
67. **The scoreboard itself** (have). Public, by state and district, updated daily. Nobody wants to be the last district in the county on the red list.
68. **Districts that stopped** (have, page). The list of who ended it and when, with the policy text. The norm message in one page.

## 10. Research, evidence, and institutions

69. **The outcome study nobody has done** (build, person). No published study measures what happened after a district or state banned it. The record plus CRDC is the dataset. A university partner makes it citable, and a citable study is a lever for every audience above.
70. **The AAP, NASP, AACAP positions delivered locally** (have, claims). The positions exist; delivery to the local chapter and the local board is the tactic.
71. **The research dossier for the board that relied on nothing** (have). Texas's own records show no research basis. Ask every state the same question.

## 11. Automation and scale

72. **Ground Crew recruiting** (have, server). Record-completion and neighbor research as tasks any agent can claim. The map fills itself.
73. **The experiment engine** (have, v1). Every message kind runs as A against B; wins measured at 14 and 30 days; a weekly review proposes the next variants.
74. **Custom video per district** (build). The film with the district's own numbers and name in the last scene, generated from the record. Sent to the board.
75. **The always-on engine** (staged). Nothing above matters if the cycle hangs on a laptop for a week.
76. **Agent-to-agent** (have, connect page). Superintendents' own AI assistants can read the record through the crew server. "Ask your assistant to check your district on earthpilot.org/kids" is a tactic.

## What we measure for every tactic

Reply within 7 days; a document or recorded status within 14; a board agenda item, a policy flip, or a certificate within 30 and 90; and the score. Clicks on tagged links are the early signal. Every move is a line in `data/game/moves.jsonl`; every result a line in `outcomes.jsonl`.

## Lines we do not cross

- Honest persuasion only. No fake residents, no invented stories, no pressure a superintendent would be embarrassed to see in print. Superintendents in a state talk to each other.
- No minor is ever named, quoted, or depicted.
- Officials' public positions are documented; their private lives are not. No contact with families or employers.
- Every new message kind queues held; Anthony sees the first batch before it sends. One touch per district per ten business days.
- Every number comes from `facts/claims/` with its year.
