Tennessee

Legal in public schools

1,031 students in 2021-22; 616 in 2023-24 (down 40 percent, after the 2023 documentation law). No bill in the 2025-26 session by caption search. Jackson County: a teacher and assistant principal were indicted in November 2023 after two paddlings injured a student; outcome not found.

The law

T.C.A. § 49-6-4103 (as amended by Public Chapter 275, 2023), § 49-6-4104 text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • Before corporal punishment the educator must document having addressed the behavior, provided consequences, consulted the parent, and considered whether to evaluate the student for a disability (Public Chapter 275, effective April 28, 2023)
  • No corporal punishment on a student with a disability unless district policy permits it and the parent gives written permission specifying type and circumstances, with notice each time (HB 2330, 2018)
  • Each local board adopts rules to implement and control any form of corporal punishment (§ 49-6-4104)

Bills

  • SB 972 / HB 995 (Pub. Ch. 275) (2023), Sen. Bill Powers (R), Rep. John Stevens (R); co-primes Sens. Akbari, Campbell: Documentation prerequisites before corporal punishment; Senate 32-0, House 90-0, signed April 28, 2023 signed record
  • SB 1788 / HB 1965 (2024), Sen. Heidi Campbell (D), Rep. Jason Powell (D): Full ban removing statutory authorization; SB 1788 failed for lack of a second in Senate Education March 13, 2024; HB 1965 taken off notice failed record
