Florida

Legal in public schools

17 of 67 districts reported 516 instances in 2023-24 (Suwannee 74, Holmes 65, Calhoun 59, Columbia 50, Wakulla 45, Washington 43); 647 students in the 2021-22 federal count. Districts that still permit it are coded consent_required. The consent law came from a four-year University of Florida student campaign after a disability-only ban was blocked. Hendry County banned it in 2021 after a principal was filmed paddling a six-year-old.

The law

Fla. Stat. § 1003.32(1)(k) (teacher authority per board policy) and § 1002.20(4)(c) (as amended by CS/HB 1255, 2025, ch. 2025-110) text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • Since July 1, 2025 a board that authorizes it must require parental consent, for the school year or before each instance (the statute says 'consent,' not 'written consent'); the board must review the policy at a public meeting every three years or the authorization expires; charters must comply (§ 1002.33(16)(b)17)
  • Principal approval in principle and principal guidelines; another adult present and informed of the reason; written explanation to the parent on request (§ 1003.32(1)(k))
  • No disability ban

Bills

  • HB 109 / SB 662 (2026), Rep. Johanna López (D), Sen. Mack Bernard (D): Full ban for district employees; died in committee March 13, 2026 failed record
  • CS/HB 1255 (2025), Reps. Dana Trabulsy and Chase Tramont (R): Omnibus education bill adding the parental-consent requirement; final votes 100-0 and 30-0; signed May 30, 2025 signed record
  • HB 439 / SB 1318 (2024), Reps. Waldron, Beltran, Garcia, Gottlieb, Stark; Sen. Thompson: Remove teacher authority, require notification, ban for specified students; favorable in subcommittee, died March 8, 2024 failed record
