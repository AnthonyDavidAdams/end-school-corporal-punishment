Oklahoma

Legal in public schools

1,458 students in 2021-22 (991 in 2023-24); fourth-highest rate. American Indian/Alaska Native students were 24 percent of those struck in 2017-18. Bills carried by Republicans without an outside coalition. Note the statutory bar on State Board rulemaking: the Kentucky regulation route is unavailable here without a statute change.

The law

70 O.S. § 24-100.4(C) (teacher has a parent's right to discipline 'according to district policies'; formerly § 6-114); 21 O.S. § 844 ('spanking, switching or paddling'); 70 O.S. § 13-116 (disability ban, SB 364, 2025) text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • No corporal punishment on any student identified with a disability under IDEA (SB 364, Laws 2025 ch. 99; law without the governor's signature May 8, 2025; effective about August 28, 2025 by constitutional default since the emergency clause failed)
  • 70 O.S. § 24-100.4(D): the State Board may not proscribe corporal punishment except for IEP students, so Kentucky's regulatory route is closed by statute in Oklahoma
  • No statewide consent, witness, instrument or record rules

Bills

  • SB 364 (2025), Sen. Dave Rader and Rep. Anthony Moore (R): Ban corporal punishment for any student with an IDEA disability; Senate 31-16, House 63-25; became law without Gov. Stitt's signature signed record
  • HB 2244 (2025): Reporting requirement; stalled failed record
  • HB 1028 (2023-24), Rep. John Talley (R): Disability ban; failed 45-43 March 14, 2023, passed 84-8 on reconsideration March 20, passed both chambers in different forms, died failed record
