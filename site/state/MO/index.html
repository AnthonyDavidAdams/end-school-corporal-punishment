Missouri

Legal in public schools

At least 123 districts authorized corporal punishment for 2022-23 (Columbia Missourian, April 2023). Cassville R-IV reinstated opt-in paddling in August 2022. 1,133 students in 2021-22; 1,002 in 2023-24. Ban bills have never received a hearing.

The law

RSMo 160.261 (as amended by SBs 681 and 662, 2022) text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • No pupil may be subject to corporal punishment without the parent being notified and providing written permission (opt-in, effective August 28, 2022)
  • Spanking must be administered by certificated personnel in the presence of a district-employee witness to be exempt from the abuse definition (160.261.10)
  • Each board's written discipline policy must state its determination on corporal punishment

Bills

  • HB 2827 (2026), Rep. Jo Doll (D): Require every district policy to prohibit corporal punishment; referred May 15, 2026, no hearing failed record
  • HB 1581 (2024), Rep. Maggie Nurrenbern (D): Require district policies to prohibit corporal punishment; no hearing failed record
  • HB 121 (2023), Rep. Maggie Nurrenbern (D): Require district policies to prohibit corporal punishment; no hearing failed record
  • HB 160 (2023), Rep. Ian Mackey (D): Require district policies to prohibit corporal punishment; no hearing failed record
