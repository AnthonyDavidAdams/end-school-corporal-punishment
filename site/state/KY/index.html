Kentucky

Legal, but every district has stopped

All 171 districts had prohibiting policies by November 2, 2023; zero incidents in 2023-24 (5 in 2022-23, 142 in 2019-20, about 1,100 in 2011-12). The statute still permits it, and advocates want a ban in law because policies can be reversed.

The law

KRS 503.110 (permits); 704 KAR 7:170 (regulates) text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • Under 704 KAR 7:170 (effective Aug 30, 2022) a district that permits it must obtain annual written and per-instance verbal parental consent, use a same-gender certified witness, no instruments, provide counseling by the next school day, and may not use it on students with IEPs or 504 plans, homeless or foster youth

Bills

  • HB 22 (2020 Regular Session), Rep. Steve Riley (R): Ban corporal punishment in schools; passed House 65-17, died in Senate Education failed record
  • HB 119 (2022 Regular Session), Rep. Steve Riley (R): Ban corporal punishment in schools; died in committee failed record
