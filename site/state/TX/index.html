Texas

Legal in public schools

Largest absolute use: 7,324 students in 2021-22 and 5,359 in 2023-24; 13,892 in 2017-18. IDRA counted 468 districts with corporal punishment policies in 2017-18. Overton ISD, 2023: a principal's assault charge for a bruising paddling was dismissed because district policy was followed and the mother consented. Millsap ISD, 2025: indictments over staff striking an autistic student (abuse, not sanctioned paddling).

The law

Tex. Educ. Code § 37.0011 (added 2011, HB 359) text

Private schools are not covered.

Limits

  • If the student code of conduct permits it, a district educator may use corporal punishment unless the parent has provided a signed written statement prohibiting it, renewed each school year (opt-out)
  • Tex. Educ. Code § 22.0512 bars districts from disciplining an employee for force justified under Penal Code § 9.62

Bills

  • HB 1415 (89R (2025)), Rep. Alma Allen (D): Prohibit corporal punishment (new § 37.901); died in Public Education without a hearing failed record
  • HB 3025 (89R (2025)), Rep. Aicha Davis (D): Affirmative parental consent before corporal punishment; died without a hearing failed record
  • HB 1096 (89R (2025)), Rep. Vikki Goodwin (D): Reporting and opt-out notice; died without a hearing failed record
  • HB 772 (88R (2023)), Rep. Alma Allen (D): Ban corporal punishment in public schools; committee 5-2, failed on second reading April 26, 2023 (reported 58-86); Allen has filed it every session for 18 years failed record
