Louisiana

Legal in public schools

Louisiana is the clearest opt-in state: since August 2023 a school may not paddle without the parent's signed annual consent on the state form. A 2022 survey found 27 systems prohibiting and 19 allowing of 46 responding. 264 students in 2021-22; 65 in 2023-24. No bill in 2025 or 2026.

The law

La. R.S. 17:416.1(B) (public; Acts 2017 No. 266, 2023 No. 268, 2024 No. 780) and 17:416.22 (state-funded nonpublic schools) text

Limits

  • Prohibited in any public school unless the parent gives written consent for that school year on a form created by the state Department of Education (Act 268 of 2023, HB 242, effective August 1, 2023)
  • No corporal punishment on a student with an exceptionality (other than gifted) or an Individual Accommodation Plan (Act 266 of 2017)
  • Taping a student's mouth or otherwise restricting the airway is prohibited regardless of consent (Act 780 of 2024, HB 107)
  • The same rules apply to any nonpublic school that receives state funds (R.S. 17:416.22)

Bills

  • HB 242 (Act 268) (2023 Regular Session), Rep. Stephanie Hilferty: Written annual parental opt-in on a state form; House 74-21, Senate 37-1, signed June 9, 2023 signed record
  • HB 107 (Act 780) (2024 Regular Session), Rep. Bayham: Ban on mouth-taping and airway restriction regardless of consent; law without signature June 25, 2024 signed record
  • HB 649 (2022 Regular Session): Opt-in consent; passed the House, died at sine die (the Global Initiative wrongly reports it as passed) failed record
  • HB 324 (2021 Regular Session): Full ban; failed the House 48-49 failed record
