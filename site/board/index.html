One sentence.

A four-minute film for school board members: what the law permits, what the research finds, where the protection for staff stops, and the sentence 291 Texas boards already adopted.

“The Board prohibits the use of corporal punishment in the District.”

That is the whole policy. In Texas it is one word in FO(LOCAL), the policy code every board already has. For a district that permits corporal punishment on paper and has not used it in years, adopting the sentence changes nothing on Monday morning except the exposure.

What the research finds

Across 21 studies and 67,400 children, school corporal punishment is associated with more aggressive and disruptive behavior, more anxiety and depression, and lower school performance. Across 75 studies it did not even reliably stop the behavior in the moment. Every source is at Facts.

Where the immunity stops

Texas shields employees from personal liability except for excessive force or negligence causing bodily injury (Education Code 22.0511), and protects their job and certificate only for force a court later agrees was reasonable and necessary (22.0512, Penal Code 9.62). Those words are decided afterward, by someone else.
