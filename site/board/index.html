“The Board prohibits the use of corporal punishment in the District.”

That is the whole policy. For a district that permits corporal punishment on paper and has not used it in years, adopting the sentence changes nothing on Monday morning except the exposure. Hundreds of boards in permitting states have already put it in writing; your state's page shows which ones, in their own words.

What the research finds

Across 21 studies and 67,400 children, school corporal punishment is associated with more aggressive and disruptive behavior, more anxiety and depression, and lower school performance. Across 75 studies it did not even reliably stop the behavior in the moment. Every source is at Facts.

Where the protection stops

Every state that permits the practice shields staff only for force that follows the policy and that a court later agrees was reasonable. Where the policy is not followed, the protection is gone, and whether it was followed is decided afterward, by someone else. The statute for your state is on your state's page.

What we are asking

Put the sentence on an agenda. The two-page board brief for your district, the model language in your state's policy code, a staff training and the letters to staff and families are free at the kit. When it is adopted, the district is recognized as a Safe School, with a certificate and a press release it can use.
