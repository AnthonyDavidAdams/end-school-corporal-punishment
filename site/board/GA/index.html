One sentence.

For Georgia school board members: what the law permits, what the research finds, where the protection for staff stops, and the one sentence that ends it.

The four-minute film, Texas edition: the research and the ask are the same everywhere; the Georgia figures are below. A Georgia edition is in production.

“The Board prohibits the use of corporal punishment in the District.”

The sentence most Texas boards use. 32 Georgia boards prohibit the practice in their own words. For a district that permits corporal punishment on paper and has not used it in years, adopting the sentence changes nothing on Monday morning except the exposure. In Georgia, 49 districts are in exactly that position.

Georgia in numbers

1,127 students were struck in Georgia public schools in 2023-24. In 2017-18 it was 3,697: the practice is already leaving. Of the students struck, 81 percent were boys and 23 percent had a disability.

U.S. Department of Education Civil Rights Data Collection.

What the research finds

Across 21 studies and 67,400 children, school corporal punishment is associated with more aggressive and disruptive behavior, more anxiety and depression, and lower school performance. Across 75 studies it did not even reliably stop the behavior in the moment. Every source is at Facts.

What Georgia law says

O.C.G.A. §§ 20-2-730 to 20-2-732 permits it. The law's own conditions: Not excessive or unduly severe; not a first-line punishment unless the pupil was warned; administered in the presence of a principal, assistant principal or designee informed beforehand in the pupil's presence; written explanation to the parent on request; No corporal punishment on a child whose parent filed, on the day of enrollment, a Georgia-licensed physician's statement that it would be detrimental to the child's mental or emotional stability (§ 20-2-731). Every immunity statute runs out at the policy's edge: where the policy is not followed, the protection is gone, and whether it was followed is decided afterward, by someone else.

Who already did it

32 of the 165 Georgia boards on the public record prohibit it in writing. See every Georgia district's own policy line.
