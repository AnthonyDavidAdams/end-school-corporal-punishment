Districts that stopped

3 districts in this record reported striking students to the federal government in 2023-24 and now prohibit corporal punishment in their own board policy. Between them they struck 311 children in that one year.

Every board that still permits it has already heard the argument that it is wrong. What it has not been shown is a district of its own size, in its own state, under its own law, that stopped and is still running. These are those districts, in their own words.

The Pike County Board of Education prohibits the use of corporal punishment. No person employed by or engaged in the Pike County Schools shall administer corporal punishment upon a pupil.

Pike County Schools, policy 5.30.1 · 252 students struck in 2023-24 · read 2026-09-09

The Lafayette County School Board prohibits the use of corporal punishment by any employee of the District against any student.

What a row here means, and what it does not

A district appears here because its own current policy prohibits corporal punishment and its own federal filing for 2023-24 reported students struck. Those two facts can both be true for two different reasons, and the record for each district says which:

  • The district changed its policy after the filing. Pike County, Alabama revised policy 5.30.1 on 17 June 2024, after the school year in which it reported 252 students struck. That is a district that stopped.
  • The filing does not match a policy that was already in place. Lubbock ISD removed corporal punishment in 2020 and its 2023-24 filing still shows two students. That is a reporting question, not a change of heart, and it is on this page because hiding it would make the other rows less believable.
